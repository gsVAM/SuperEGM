# SuperEGM 1.2.1 — design

Date: 2026-11-12. Status: brainstormed with the owner; in `/superegm:review-spec`.

Inputs: the owner's 1.2.1 change list (2026-11-12, given in conversation; this design is its record);
the 1.2 integrate review (`spec-2026-11-03-superegm-design-integrate-c1`, 0 high, 7 medium, 8 low,
deferred to 1.2.1 by the owner on 2026-11-10; §8 restates each finding it settles); the 1.0 build's
unit sizes and launch counts (§6); the 1.2 design,
[docs/specs/2026-11-03-superegm-design.md](2026-11-03-superegm-design.md), which this design amends
and does not restate. Where this design contradicts a passage of the 1.2 design, the passage is named
in §2.1 and this design wins. A term this design uses without defining it is the 1.2 design's (1.2
§2, Vocabulary). "1.2 §n" means the 1.2 design's section n; "§n" means this design's.

Owner's answers in the brainstorm and in this review: cap default 3, a spec's `Rounds:` line
overriding it for its own handoff; triage rules in the spec, highs never eligible; instructions for
builders, the foreman, reviewers and judges; the chain in both modes, on for a store whose owner agrees in the SDLC workflow step (§3.4) and off for a 1.2
store until the owner turns it on; the size limits of §6; SuperEGM's own builders as in §10.

## 1. Intent

1.2 made SuperEGM runnable while the owner is away. Running it for real showed the places where
the owner still does work a machine could do, or where the fixed rules fit one project badly:

- **Every phase waits for a typed command**, even the ones that are mechanical once the spec is
  settled: plan, plan review, file, wave. The owner's judgement is spent on the spec; after
  approval it should be spent only on the decisions the workflow raises.
- **Five review rounds is too many for most work**, and the number is a constant.
- **Severity alone decides what blocks.** A medium that only a hand-forged in-process object can
  trigger, and that is easy to fix later, blocks a merge as hard as one an HTTP caller can trigger.
  Each project wants its own rule, and the rule must be written down before the build, not argued
  per finding.
- **Units are too big.** The 1.0 build, which had no body limit, filed unit bodies of 59–94 KB,
  22–52 files and 26–60 steps that took up to 11 launches each; a 24 KB, 8-file, 16-step unit merged
  first time. 1.2's 48 KiB body limit refuses every one of the large ones, but still passes a unit
  twice U01's size, a band no build has tried.

- **A narrowed panel can never finish.** A cycle's panel is fixed when it opens (1.2 §8). An owner
  who narrows the panel mid-cycle can only record the dropped reviewers `--not-run`, which
  makes every later round `not run`: the cycle neither converges nor reaches its cap, and two such
  rounds on one run card stop it as stalled (`review.py:176-186`, `:867-888`). The 1.2.1 spec's own
  review hit this at round 5.
- **A builder's tooling is set up by hand and first tested by a real launch.** `init` prints the
  builder profile and `builder.mcp` as by-hand steps. A builder runs in its own profile with
  `--strict-mcp-config`, so the servers, hooks and connectors of the owner's own sessions never
  reach it. Nothing checks that a server in `builder.mcp` connects, that it is authenticated in the
  builder profile, or that a start-of-session command an instruction file asks for (a Relay
  registration, §3.2) passes the allow-list. The owner finds out from a failed launch.
- **The wave phase takes days.** The 1.2 build's store (`~/.cache/superegm/build-1.2`) ran its wave
  phase for about 90 hours for 15 units, through 84 builder launches of about 36 minutes each
  (5.6 per unit, U22 12). No builder ran for 48 of those hours, one ran for 38, and two or more for
  about 5, with five allowed. The owner was not the bottleneck: half of the 44 decisions were
  answered within about 6 minutes, 23 hours of decision wait in all, mostly overnight. Two rules
  cost the rest. A unit waits until every unit before it on a shared file has landed (1.2 §5), so
  the plan's 15 units ran in 12 waves; 1.2.1's own first 25 units come out at 9 waves under that rule, seven
  of them one or two units wide, and at 5 waves without it. And every launch after a unit's first starts a fresh builder that
  reads the unit from scratch (1.2 §5), though almost every such launch is a fix after one review.
  The idle hours include nights, and they come from one wave run.

And one place where the owner pastes the same text by hand: repository rules that every builder,
reviewer and judge should see (a house standard, a registration step) go into every issue body today.

And the owner is the messenger. A ruling, an undo or a wait answered before filing takes a
`**Ruling:**` comment typed on GitHub or a phase skill typed in a new session, and the inbox prints
the text to paste rather than doing it. A finished builder waits up to 15 minutes for the next tick
to notice it.

The later goals answer problems the 1.2 builds showed. Plans pin more than a builder needs (§6.5's
numbers), and a unit's size (its body, Scope paths and steps) has no limit a repository sets (§6). Rules and reference documents
reach an agent only when someone pastes them by hand, and the planner and reviewers see different
copies. The reviewer panels are fixed: a repository cannot name its own reviewer or retune a
built-in. Nothing is learnt between builds: the store holds every round, ruling and wait, and no
step reads it back into the spec, the reference documents or the workflow. A spec shown wrong
mid-build costs a void and a full re-run, however small the fault. A builder's contest of a foreman
finding is put to an owner who has not seen the diff. And the brainstorm stops short of the depth
the earlier superpowers model reached, with no check by a builder before the spec is frozen.

1.2.1 fixes those, and settles the integrate review's deferred findings.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals:

1. In a store the owner registered and set `driver.chain` to `true` in, from the approval commit at
   `origin/<base>` to the first builder launch, nothing waits for a typed command except a decision
   the workflow raises (§7). In normal mode file's Create is one (1.2 §13.2 point 4), so the
   normal-mode chain always stops once before the first wave.
2. The review round cap is configuration, default 3, and a spec may set its own (§4).
3. A spec may declare the patterns under which a medium stops blocking; on every pull-request head
   with an eligible medium a triage goldfish answers fixed triage fields, and a match defers the
   medium (§5).
4. One configuration list routes repository instruction files to builders, the foreman, reviewers
   and judges, frozen at approval and recorded on every dispatch (§3).
5. Unit size limits are configuration with defaults taken from the 1.0 build; the plan splits, and
   `scan` refuses (§6).
6. Each deferred integrate finding is fixed or settled in this design (§8).
7. An owner who re-types a spec review with a narrower panel (the config list or `--reviewers`)
   narrows the open cycle from its next unrecorded round, and those rounds can complete, converge
   and reach the cap (§4.1).
8. The owner can end any review at any round with a `proceed` ruling, highs, stalls and moved bytes
   included; the ruling stands and the workflow goes on (§4.2).
9. One helper sets up the builder's profile, MCP servers and allow rules from what the owner already
   uses, and `doctor --probe` runs one builder-shaped session that shows whether they work (§3.3).
10. Units that share a file build at the same time: a unit is ready once the units it is blocked by
    have landed, the shared-file order becomes the merge order, and a unit is relaunched only when
    its pull request really conflicts with the base (§6.1).
11. A builder's later rounds resume its earlier session instead of starting a fresh one, with a
    fresh session whenever the resume cannot be trusted (§6.2).
12. A brainstorm that ends in a spec goes on to design the SDLC workflow: reviewers, instructions
    and reference documents, the round cap, the mode, the builder, doctor and the chain, so a broken
    setup shows before the spec review rather than at `plan` or `wave` (§3.4).
13. A builder checks its own work before it pushes: the foreman's worktree checks, the DoD runs and
    one self-review, fixed and re-run in the same session, so a pull-request-head round starts
    from evidence rather than finding what the builder could have found (§6.3).
14. A unit whose head reviews keep failing gets the stronger model on its next round (§6.4).
15. The owner answers every question in words in the inbox, each led by the agent's pick so a "yes"
    is enough: SuperEGM posts the ruling, records the
    answer, merges on the owner's word and goes on; no step asks the owner to post a comment or
    paste a command (§7.3).
16. A store whose owner agrees in the SDLC workflow step (§3.4) has the chain on, and a builder's exit or the owner's answer starts
    the next tick at once instead of at the next interval (§7.4).
17. An external reviewer's comment reaches the builder as its text, not its first line, and hidden
    markup never stands in for the text (§5.3).
18. A plan pins what binds a builder and leaves how to build it to the builder. A builder's
    evidence that the brief is wrong is settled by one ruling, and a finding waiting on a ruling is
    posted once and stays open until ruled (§6.5).
19. A repository can list a folder of reference documents; every agent gets an index of them and
    reads one when its work touches it, and the brainstorm offers to draft them from the spec (§3.5).
20. The owner can add review goldfish, reword a built-in one and choose per kind which run, by one
    file per reviewer in the repository, frozen at approval (§3.6).
21. A finished build leaves one post-mortem, written from the store, of where the rounds, the
    findings and the owner's time went (§7.5).
22. The brainstorm runs superpowers' builder check on the spec before handing it over and offers a
    browser companion for what is easier seen than read (§3.7).
23. When the build shows the spec wrong, the owner corrects the one section in place: a narrowed
    review of the diff alone, an approval on the same handoff, and only the units the change touches
    rebuilt (§7.6).
24. Each finished build improves the next: the post-mortem's lessons become, through a Q&A with the
    owner, edits to reference documents, reviewers, configuration and the brainstorm, which the next
    spec's approval freezes (§7.5, §7.7).
25. A builder's contest of a foreman finding on a head goes to the judge panel first in both modes;
    the owner rules only what the panel does not converge on (§7.8).

Non-goals:

- Merging without the owner. The driver never merges, in either mode; the inbox merges only on the
  owner's word in an interactive session (§7.3).
- Triage on spec, plan and integrate reviews, and on highs. Those are unchanged.
- A per-finding override of the triage rules. A rule change is a spec change and needs a new
  approval.
- Registering a store, or turning its chain on, without the owner. The SDLC workflow step sets `driver.chain`
  only on the owner's yes (§3.4); `superegm register`, the env file and `driver install` run only on the owner's yes, from `review-spec`'s approval step or a typed `/superegm:driver` (§3.4), and a store set up under 1.2 keeps its chain off until the owner turns it on.
- Asking the owner a question without the agent's pick. Every question SuperEGM puts to the owner
  (the inbox, the SDLC walk, a respec or retrospective Q&A) leads with the suggested answer, so a yes
  is enough. The owner's non-design steps kept on purpose are the merge's word (§7.3), normal-mode
  `file` Create and `kill_failed`; each is a step whose consequence leaves the repository.
- Remote builder hosts (the AgentCore line of work, builders launched on a hosted runtime). The
  launch contract is unchanged.
- A chain that reacts to a pull request's change (a CI result, a comment posted on GitHub). Those
  wait for the tick (§7.4); only a builder's exit, a driven phase's exit and the owner's inbox
  answer kick it.

Anti-goals:

1. **A high never stops blocking by triage.** Triage covers mediums only; a high is never triaged
   or deferred.
2. **No instruction reaches an agent unrecorded.** Every dispatch whose brief or prompt carried an
   instruction file records the file and its blob sha in its own `instructions[]` field (§3.1). The
   isolation field still comes from the agent's `saw:` line alone, so a hook's exposure stays
   visible on its own.
3. **SuperEGM reads no instruction or ruling from chat or Relay.** An instruction file is a
   committed file; rulings come only from the `**Ruling:**` grammar from `config.owners`, or from the
   owner in an interactive session, which includes an answer the inbox recorded there (§7.3).

### 2.1 Passages of the 1.2 design this design supersedes

- 1.2 §3's non-goal "Chaining phases", 1.2 §2's **driver** row ("chains nothing"; "a tick types
  `wave --leavemealone`") and 1.2 §11.2's launch of that command: where `driver.chain` is `true`,
  the driver chains plan, review-plan, file and wave, and passes `--leavemealone` by the frozen mode
  (§7.1). It never types `integrate`. With the chain off, 1.2's driver is unchanged. The same holds
  for 1.2 §11.2's "A tick types one phase and chains nothing", its "It never types `integrate`,
  `plan`, `review-plan` or `file`", its driver-role line "a decision the judges did not converge on
  is a journal line" (a chain phase writes a wait record or an open decision instead, §7.2), and
  1.2 §11's road back, "each typed and nothing chained".
- The "one human step" paragraph after 1.2 §5's phase table ("the owner then types the next phase;
  nothing chains"): with the chain on, the push stays the owner's and the next phase is the driver's.
  The push is followed by the owner's checkout of `<base>` at the pushed commit; until then `next`
  prints `waiting checkout` (§7.1), and `review-spec`'s closing line names both steps (U58).
- 1.2 §4.1's risk rows "a phase never chains into the next" and "the driver types one phase …
  chains nothing": replaced by §9's chain rows.
- 1.2 §13.2's leavemealone source ("`--leavemealone` or `config.mode`"): for a handoff approved
  under 1.2.1, the mode frozen in its approval entry (§7.1).
- 1.2 §3's anti-goal 3, the closed list of what a builder sees: it gains the instruction files
  addressed to `builder` (§3).
- 1.2 §3's goal "every Claude goldfish and judge is isolated by construction": it still holds for
  the adapter; a goldfish or judge now also reads the instruction files addressed to it, recorded per
  dispatch (anti-goal 2).
- 1.2 §2's **review panel** row ("Fixed for every round") and 1.2 §8's "The panel cannot change
  after this" for spec reviews: a later round may run a narrower panel (§4.1); it never runs a wider one.
- The cap of five rounds (1.2 §5 and 1.2 §4.1): the cap is `review.rounds` or the spec's `Rounds:`
  line (§4).
- 1.2 §5's "a round is clean when no high or medium finding is unsettled": on a head, a medium
  deferred by triage counts as settled (§5.2).
- 1.2 §9.1's unit body limit of 49,152 bytes and its 24,576-byte warning, and 1.2 §9.2's split check
  (over 10 Steps, 24 KB or eight Scope paths): replaced by `units.*` (§6).
- The judges' order of 1.2 §13.1 and 1.2 §17: the landed order fable, opus, astra (§8, design-1-4).
- 1.2's refusals of an owner's go-ahead (accept below the cap with a high open, accept on a head at
  the cap, accept after the reviewed bytes moved, a stalled cycle with no way on but a new round):
  the owner's `proceed` ruling passes all of them (§4.2). `accept` itself keeps 1.2's rules.
- 1.2 §17 U35's accept below the cap (point 5 here), which states no lapse: the landed lapse rule
  (§8, design-1-3).
- 1.2 §5's "Dependency readiness means landed" (a unit waits for every shared-file predecessor to
  land) and 1.2 §9.1's "Wave width is set by file overlap alone": for a handoff approved under 1.2.1, readiness and waves come from `Blocked
  by` alone and the shared-file order is the merge order (§6.1). The `width:` line is unchanged: a
  long shared-file chain is still a long merge sequence. The cross-handoff conflict at the
  owner's merge (1.2 §9.1, "resolved by hand at the fold") now relaunches the conflicting unit
  instead (§6.1).
- 1.2 §5's "The builder is always a fresh session": a later round resumes the unit's earlier
  session where §6.2 allows, and is fresh otherwise.
- 1.2 §9.1's rule that a Step's fence holds "the test signature and assertions", 1.2 §9.2's
  readiness question (whether the builder knows exactly what to code, where and how) and its
  "a blocking question is high", and the handoff standard's matching severity paragraph: a plan pins contracts and acceptance values, not harness
  or private detail, and readiness asks whether a builder who reads the code would have to make a
  decision it cannot make (§6.5).
- 1.2 §10's deviation kinds and 1.2 §5's rule that the builder's only brief is the frozen body and the
  rulings: kinds are defined by their effect, and an owner's `amend` ruling restates a producing
  unit's Interfaces line without touching the filed bytes (§6.5).

## 3. Repository instructions

### 3.1 The key

```json
"instructions": [
  {"file": ".superegm/rules.md", "to": ["builder", "foreman", "reviewers", "judges"]}
]
```

- `instructions` (default `[]`) is a list of entries. `file`: a path relative to the repository
  root, at most 8,192 bytes. `to`: a non-empty list from `builder`, `foreman`, `reviewers`,
  `judges`. Registered by the new module `skills/plan/scripts/superegm/instructions.py`.
- **Frozen at approval.** `approve-spec` copies the list into the approval entry with each file's
  12-hex blob sha at the approval commit, and refuses an entry whose file is absent there or over
  the size limit. It also copies each file into the store as
  `instructions/<blob sha>.json` (`file`, `sha`, `text`; a new store-level kind, beside
  `approvals`, and refuses a file that is not UTF-8), verified against the sha, so the frozen bytes outlive an amended or
  squash-merged approval commit that git later prunes; every reader below reads that copy. It prints each file whose sha differs from the spec's previous approval entry in
  `approvals/<slug>.json`, whatever its handoff, and every file with its sha when the spec has no
  earlier entry, so a merged edit to a file is seen when it takes effect. An `--amend-units` entry copies the list
  and its shas unchanged (§6), so the files are the same for the whole handoff. A later config edit
  governs the next approval only.
- **Where it is read.** Every role of an approved handoff reads each file by its frozen blob sha (the
  approval commit itself is not needed): a builder, the foreman, head reviewers, plan reviewers,
  integrate reviewers and every judge panel ruling on them. Nothing a builder commits on its branch
  or pull request reaches any brief. A spec review, its reviewers and any judge panel ruling on it
  read the config and the files at the commit `HEAD` names when the brief is rendered, whether or not
  the spec was approved before. The triage goldfish (§5.2) reads none: it answers fixed fields
  against the code.
- **One renderer.** `superegm instructions --role <role> [--lane local|astra] (--handoff <id> | --spec <path> --review <id>
  --round <n>)` prints the section. Its source is one function in `instructions.py` that returns
  `(manifest, text)` for a role, from a handoff's frozen list or from `HEAD`, and stores nothing; the
  command, the card and round stores below, and `judges.py` all call it. Exactly one of `--handoff` and
  `--spec` is required. `--handoff <id>` reads the frozen list of the last approval entry whose `handoff_id` is `<id>`, as `launch.py:46` finds it (`[]` with no entry) and each file from
  its store copy by sha. `--spec` reads the list from the config committed at `HEAD` (a config `HEAD` lacks, as before a
  repository's first approval, reads `[]`) and the files there, refuses
  an entry whose file `HEAD` lacks, and stores the manifest it rendered (`[{file, sha}]`) and the
  rendered text in `reviews/<id>/instructions-<n>-<role>-<lane>.json` (`{manifest, text}`, `<lane>` `local` by default; a name
  `round_numbers` does not glob, so `review record`'s first-record and cap checks,
  `review.py:501-529`, are unchanged). `--handoff` requires `--runcard <card>` (`require_runcard`, `__init__.py:666-687`) and takes an
  optional `--unit <U>`, so a unit's card (the foreman's child card, artifact the unit,
  `foreman.py:87`) passes the artifact check (`subcommand_artifact`, `__init__.py:656-663`); the
  foreman renders the head reviewers' section under its child card with `--unit`, the card its
  reviewers' records copy from. It stores
  `{approved_at, manifest, text}`, the approval entry it read included, on that card for that role and lane.
  Only the first call for a round, role and lane, or a card, role and lane, renders, and each record copies its own lane's manifest; a later call (a retry, a
  second reviewer of the role) prints the stored text whatever `HEAD` or the latest approval is
  then, so a dispatch's manifest never changes. The section is one `# Repository instructions` heading, then each
  file addressed to that role, in list order, under a `## <file>` line, its text verbatim inside a
  fence of backticks one longer than the longest backtick run in the file (at least three), placed
  last in the brief or prompt. With no file addressed to the role it prints nothing. `launch-builder`
  and `prepare.py`'s `astra_brief` call the same function; the elephant and the foreman paste its
  output into the briefs they write. The section is part of the brief: an agent does not name it on
  its `saw:` line, and the isolation parser is unchanged. An Astra round n > 1 of any review, spec or
  plan, resumes the previous round's session only when the round's rendered manifest for Astra
  equals the `instructions[]` recorded on round n-1's Astra record (a file added, removed or
  changed, a recipient changed, or no such record, launches fresh and says so), an added condition
  to the resume test of `review_loop.md:107-110`; a resumed session would otherwise keep a file the
  round's record does not name. A plan review resumed under a new card after a same-id re-approval
  (`approve_spec.py:62-68`, `review_plan.py:71-84`) is the case this covers.
- **Recorded.** Every dispatch stores `instructions: [{file, sha}]`; no caller passes it. A dispatch with no
  stored render records `[]`: no section reached it. For an
  approved handoff the recording helper copies the manifest stored on the dispatch's card for its
  role (a re-approval after the dispatch does not change it). A spec review's `review record` copies
  the manifest stored for that round, role and lane. Neither resolves `HEAD` or the latest approval again.
  A judge panel renders once per lane (its local members' and its `astra` member's), when it opens, by its parent card's skill first: a `review-spec`
  parent always renders the card's artifact as the spec at `HEAD` (its card can carry a legacy
  store's handoff id, `__init__.py:428-432`, `:486`, which is not the spec's); any other parent
  renders its card's handoff's frozen list (`__init__.py:482-486`), nothing for a card with no
  handoff (`__init__.py:475-477`); a panel with no parent card
  (a typed `/superegm:judges`, `judges.py:238-241`) renders nothing and stores an empty list; `render_brief`
  stores the manifest and text on the panel, and every later panel round (`judges.py:245`, `:425`)
  reuses the stored text. It lands on the builder's launch entry, each review round's reviewer record, the
  judge panel's member record, and the wave's record of the foreman's own dispatch in
  `foreman/<unit>.json`; an empty list when none was addressed.
- **Tools stay where they are.** A file cannot grant a tool: a command it asks the builder to run
  still needs a `builder.allowed_tools` entry the owner adds.
- `pr_block_file` is unchanged: it is text for the pull request body, read at the launch's ref, not
  an instruction.
- **Off the machine.** The Astra lane (the Codex-backed reviewer, 1.2 §8) and the `astra` judge
  member send their brief to the Codex vendor, so a file addressed to `reviewers` or `judges` goes
  there too; `docs/privacy.md` says so.

### 3.2 First user

The owner's first file is addressed to `builder` only: a one-time registration command at the start
of each builder session (Relay, the owner's agent-messaging service). It is the owner's file, not
part of this design; the owner adds the command's prefix to `builder.allowed_tools`. Subagents
(reviewers, judges, the foreman) are never addressed with it: a registration names a session's
process. The risk of what such a channel then delivers is §9's. The owner's SessionStart
registration hook in their own profile never reaches a builder (§3.3), so the instruction file is
the builder's only path. `builder-setup --mcp <server> --allow <register command>` writes the config,
and `doctor --probe` shows the registration run in a builder-shaped process.

### 3.3 Builder setup and the probe

A builder is its own `claude -p` process in the builder profile (1.2 §11). It loads only
`builder.mcp`, by `--mcp-config --strict-mcp-config`, and the profile may hold no hooks
(`launch_builder.py:347-362`). So nothing the owner sets up in their own profile reaches it: an MCP
server, a SessionStart hook (for example one that registers each session on Relay), or a
connector. Subagents a builder spawns run in its process and share what it has. SuperEGM knows no
particular server; the setup and the probe are generic.

- **`superegm builder-setup [--mcp <name>]... [--allow <prefix>]...`.** It makes three changes and
  never touches the profile's login:
  - **The profile.** It creates `builder.profile_dir` when absent, and adds
    `"syncClaudeAiPlugins": false` and `"disableClaudeAiConnectors": true` to its `settings.json`,
    keeping every other key. A profile that `profile_problem` refuses for any other reason is
    refused, with that reason.
  - **`--mcp <name>`.** It copies that server's entry into `.superegm/config.json`'s `builder.mcp`
    and adds `mcp__<name>__*` to `builder.allowed_tools`. The entry comes from the owner's
    servers in `.claude.json` under `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, else `~/.claude.json`: first the
    local-scope `projects[<repository root>].mcpServers`, then the user-scope `mcpServers`; when
    absent there, from the repository's `.mcp.json`. A name in neither
    is refused. An entry is refused when any value under its `env` or `headers` is not a whole
    `${VAR}` reference, and the refusal names the key: 1.2 keeps secrets out of the config.
  - **`--allow <prefix>`.** It adds `Bash(<prefix>:*)` to `builder.allowed_tools`. Examples are
    the command a builder-addressed instruction file asks the builder to run, or a command the
    builder needs (a probe's `relay`). The rule passes `allowed_tools`' own check
    (`launch_builder.py:375-382`).

  Every addition is skipped when already present. The config change is an ordinary edit for the
  owner to commit. It prints what it changed and ends with `then run superegm doctor --probe`.
- **`superegm doctor --probe`.** After doctor's own lines, it runs one headless session in a fresh
  temporary directory. The session uses the builder launch's own flags, taken from the one
  function `command_line` builds from: profile, setting sources, plugin directories, the MCP file
  from `builder.mcp`, `ENABLE_CLAUDEAI_MCP_SERVERS=false`, the unset names, the allow-list,
  permission mode and `builder.model`. It adds `--output-format stream-json --verbose --max-turns 5`
  and uses no token, issue or worktree. The prompt is the builder-addressed instruction text, from
  U37's renderer over HEAD's `instructions` list, followed by `Do only what these instructions ask
  at the start of a session, then reply ok.`; with no such file, it is `Reply ok.` The probe prints:
  - one `probe: mcp <name> <status>` line per server, from the session's init event. `connected`
    passes. Any other status fails; `needs-auth` adds the one-time step `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR=<profile>
    claude --mcp-config <profile>/probe-mcp.json --strict-mcp-config`, then `/mcp` and
    Authenticate; the probe keeps that copy of its MCP file in the profile, since its temporary
    directory is removed.
  - one `probe: tool <name> ok|error: <first line>` line per tool call; an error fails.
  - one `probe: denied <tool> <input>` line per permission denial (the result's
    `permission_denials`); each fails, and the line names the `--allow` that would admit it.
  - a closing `probe: session ok | exit <n>`.

  Any failure makes doctor's exit non-zero. It writes nothing to the store and removes the
  temporary directory. Without `--probe`, doctor makes no model call, as in 1.2.

### 3.4 Design the SDLC workflow

In 1.2 a missing or broken setup surfaces only at `plan` or `wave`. 1.2.1 adds a second step to the
brainstorm: once the spec is written, the owner designs the workflow that will review and build it.
It comes second, not first, because most of what is configured follows from the design: which
reviewers a spec like this needs, which rules and reference documents every agent should carry,
how many rounds a review gets. Before the spec exists those choices are guesses.

- **When.** The brainstorming skill (`skills/brainstorming/SKILL.md`) runs this step after it has
  written and committed the spec and the builder check's edits (§3.7) and before it names `/superegm:review-spec`, only for a brainstorm
  that ended in a spec. A quick, clear task ("First: What Do You Actually Know?") skips it. In
  Cowork, where the helper cannot run, the skill only says once that `plan` and `wave` need
  `superegm doctor` to pass.
- **The walk.** With no `.superegm/config.json`, the skill offers `superegm init` and, once the
  owner agrees, runs it and walks the owner through the by-hand steps it prints. Then it walks each
  area below in this order, showing the current value and a suggestion drawn from the spec, and
  changes only what the owner agrees to:
  1. Reviewers: `reviewers.spec`, `reviewers.plan`, `reviewers.head` and `reviewers.integrate` (§4.1), and any custom
     reviewer the spec calls for, written as `.superegm/reviewers/<id>.md` (§3.6) and shown before
     it is written.
  2. Instructions and reference documents: the `instructions` entries (§3.1), then the reference
     documents offer (§3.5).
  3. The round cap: `review.rounds`, or a `Rounds:` line in the spec (§4).
  4. The mode: normal or leavemealone (`mode`).
  5. The builder: the owner's MCP server names (the three sources §3.3 reads), which the builder
     needs and which commands to allow, then `superegm builder-setup` (§3.3) with those `--mcp` and
     `--allow` names.
  6. `superegm doctor`, after the skill commits the config, reviewer and reference-document
     changes of areas 1 to 5, so that doctor and the probe read what `HEAD` will freeze. A doctor exit of 0 passes. Otherwise the skill shows the failing lines and
     works through each with the owner, commits each fix's changes by path before it runs doctor
     or the probe again, then runs doctor again. It offers `doctor --probe` once;
     the probe makes a model call, so it runs only when the owner agrees.
  7. The chain, once doctor passes (§7.4): on the owner's yes it sets `driver.chain` to `true`.
  The skill commits on the current branch (an uncommitted edit would leave the checkout `dirty`,
  §7.1): one commit before area 6 and one after each area-6 fix, so `approve-spec` freezes them
  with the spec, and a last one for area 7's `driver.chain` when the owner says yes, which keeps the
  checkout clean for `waiting checkout`; `driver.chain` is read live and no approval freezes it. Area 7's question also offers the driver's setup: it names the env file it would write
  (`~/.superegm/env`, mode `0600`, literal `KEY=value` lines: `PATH`, `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` and the name
  of the credential the shell holds, never its value) and the root a tick's sessions would run in
  (this checkout's repository root, on `<base>`), and the owner's yes covers both.
  The brainstorming skill writes nothing outside the repository (its `effects: working-tree` stays):
  `register` and `driver install` run under a `driver` card bound to a handoff's spec
  (`skills/driver/SKILL.md:29-43`, `register.py:25`, `driver.py:89`), which exists only once the spec is
  approved, so `/superegm:review-spec`'s approval step, typed and external, runs the setup: after
  `approve-spec` exits 0, when `driver.chain` is `true` and the store is not registered or `doctor`'s
  `driver:` line is not current, it opens a `driver` card on the new handoff's spec, runs
  `superegm driver env`, then `register` (skipped when the store is already registered, so its `root`
  never moves), then `driver install`, and closes the card.
  `superegm driver env --runcard <card> [--replace]` writes the env file from the helper's own environment,
  so the credential's value never passes through the transcript; it keeps an existing file unless
  `--replace` (the owner agreed in area 7), and refuses a `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR` whose `settings.json` names a
  `SubagentStart` hook (`env: <dir>: SubagentStart hooks`), the driver's sessions needing a profile without them.
  A refusal is shown and leaves the approval standing; the owner can type `/superegm:driver` then,
  which stays for that fallback, `reset` and `uninstall`.
- **The owner may skip.** The owner can skip any area, or the whole step. The skill records
  nothing for a skipped area and says once that `plan` and `wave` refuse until doctor passes, as
  in 1.2.
- **Nothing else changes.** `init`, `builder-setup` and `doctor` keep their own behaviour; this is
  an order of steps in the skill, and `using-superegm`'s description of the brainstorm says so in
  one line.

### 3.5 Reference documents

A repository may also keep longer reference documents (invariants, an architecture note, a domain
glossary) that agents read when the work touches them. Pasting them whole into every brief would
blow the 8,192-byte limit and every agent's context, so a folder is listed, not pasted.

- **The entry.** An `instructions` entry may name `dir` in place of `file`:
  `{"dir": "docs/reference", "to": ["builder", "foreman", "reviewers", "judges"]}`. Exactly one of
  `file` and `dir`. The folder's documents are its `*.md` files, recursively, in path order; at
  most 50 files, each at most 65,536 bytes and UTF-8. `approve-spec` refuses an entry whose folder
  is absent at the approval commit, empty, or breaks a limit, naming the file.
- **Frozen as files are.** Each document is frozen by its 12-hex blob sha exactly as §3.1 freezes a
  `file`: the approval entry lists `{dir, files: [{file, sha}]}`, and the store keeps each copy as
  `instructions/<blob sha>.json` (the same kind) and, beside it, the raw bytes as
  `instructions/<blob sha>.md`, which is what an agent reads. The changed-file print and
  `--amend-units` copy apply unchanged. A spec review reads the folder at `HEAD`, as §3.1, and
  writes the raw copies it lists into the same store paths.
- **An index, not the text.** The renderer (§3.1's one function) prints, after the role's pasted
  files, one `## Reference documents` heading and one line per document addressed to the role:
  `- <file> (<sha>): <first non-empty line, at most 120 characters> — read <absolute store path of the .md copy>`,
  followed by the sentence, verbatim: `Read a reference document when your task touches what its
  first line names; cite it by file when a finding rests on it.` The agent reads on demand with
  `Read`; nothing else in its tools changes. The manifest records each listed document as
  `{file, sha}` like a pasted file; whether an agent opened one is not recorded.
- **Off the machine.** The Astra lane and the `astra` judge member run in the repository's
  working directory and cannot read the store, so their brief carries each listed document's
  bytes pasted under its index line instead, as §3.1's pasted files are, in index order up to
  262,144 bytes for all of them together; a document past that budget keeps its index line with
  `(not pasted: over the 262,144-byte brief budget)`, so the agent knows of it by that line only,
  and the manifest records it as `{file, sha, pasted: false}`. Each pasted copy is the store's copy, checked
  against its recorded sha before pasting (a mismatch refuses the brief, naming the file), never
  the working tree's, which a builder's head may have changed, and never a commit git may prune
  or that `--amend-units` re-creates. Before the spec's first approval, a spec review's Astra brief pastes the
  `instructions/<sha>.md` copies that review wrote from `HEAD` (above), checked against their shas, and records `{file, sha}`. `docs/privacy.md` says those bytes go to the Codex vendor as
  §3.1's do. §3.1's cached rendering and its manifest are keyed by role and lane (`local` or
  `astra`), so the two lanes of one round, or the Astra member and the local members of one panel, never share a rendering.
- **The brainstorm nudges, never requires.** In the SDLC workflow step (§3.4, area 2), it offers once: draft or update the reference documents
  the spec's design implies (for example `invariants.md`, `architecture.md`), in this same session,
  under the configured `dir` or, with none, under `docs/reference/` with a `dir` entry added to
  `instructions` addressed to all four roles. On the owner's yes it drafts each document from the
  spec and the code it already read, shows each, and commits them with the step's commit, so the approval
  freezes them. A no, or silence, records nothing and the step goes on; nothing later
  checks that the documents exist.

- **Reviewed with the spec.** The reference documents the SDLC step drafts and commits (§3.4 area 2)
  are part of the spec review's evidence, so the ones a spec cycle commits are reviewed with it: `review
  scope` on a spec cycle adds every file under the configured `dir` that changed between the merge base
  with `<base>` and `HEAD` to the changed set (whole files, `changed <path>` lines), and the `design`
  reviewer reads them against the spec. A round's sha covers the spec and the manifest of those paths
  and their blob shas, so a fix to a reference document alone is a changed round, and `approve-spec`
  refuses when a document's blob differs from the converged round's manifest. A finding on one is
  settled like a finding on the spec, by an edit committed with it. A document edited outside a spec
  cycle is not reviewed; it is frozen at the next approval like any other.

### 3.6 Custom reviewers

The owner can add review goldfish of their own, rename or reword a built-in one, and choose per
kind which goldfish run.

- **One file per reviewer.** `.superegm/reviewers/<id>.md` in the repository. `<id>` matches
  `[a-z][a-z0-9-]{1,31}` and is the file's stem. The file opens with frontmatter: `name` (the
  display name, required), `kinds` (a non-empty subset of `spec`, `plan`, `head`, `integrate`,
  required), and optional `model` and `effort` in the forms `config.reviewers.model` and
  `config.reviewers.effort` take. The body after the frontmatter is the reviewer's persona, at most
  8,192 bytes, UTF-8. The new module `skills/plan/scripts/superegm/reviewers.py` loads and checks
  them; `doctor` prints one `reviewers:` line naming each loaded id and its kinds, `reviewers: none`
  when no file exists, or the first file it refuses with the reason; the line follows `mcp:`.
- **Overriding a built-in.** A file whose `<id>` is a built-in (`design`, `zoom-out`, `readiness`,
  `proportion`, `set`, `change-review`, `codebase-details`, `comprehension`, `structural`, `style`)
  replaces that reviewer's persona for this repository and keeps its id, so `FIRST` (a spec panel
  starts with `design`) and every rule naming a built-in still hold. `set` and `proportion` keep
  their briefs (`review-plan`); the override replaces the persona under them. `foreman`, `judge`,
  `cartographer` and the triage goldfish are not reviewers and cannot be overridden.
- **Choosing the panel.** `config.reviewers.<kind>` and an invocation's `--reviewers` name built-in
  and repository ids alike. `review open` refuses an id that is neither a built-in nor a loaded
  file whose `kinds` holds the review's kind (`review open: unknown reviewer <id>`, as today, or
  `review open: <id> does not review <kind>`).
- **Frozen like instructions.** `approve-spec` freezes every file under `.superegm/reviewers/` by
  blob sha into the approval entry (`reviewers: [{id, sha}]`) and the store
  (`reviewers/<blob sha>.json`: `id`, `sha`, `name`, `kinds`, `model`, `effort`, `text`), with the
  changed-file print and the `--amend-units` copy of §3.1. Plan, head and integrate reviews of a
  handoff read the frozen copies; a spec review reads `HEAD` and stores what it read on the round,
  as §3.1 does for instructions. A builder's branch never changes a reviewer.
- **Dispatch.** A built-in with no override goes through its own adapter, as today. A repository
  reviewer and an overridden built-in go through one new adapter, `superegm:repo-reviewer`
  (`.claude-plugin/agents/repo-reviewer.md`, listed in `.claude-plugin/plugin.json`'s `agents`:
  `model: inherit`, `omitClaudeMd: true`, tools `Read, Grep, Glob`; its persona
  `agents/repo-reviewer.md` says only that its persona is the brief's `# Reviewer persona` section and that it
  writes the findings-line output of every goldfish). The brief carries the frozen text under
  `# Reviewer persona`, before the artifact, and the same output section the built-ins carry, so a
  custom persona need not describe the grammar. The persona is printed by `superegm reviewers
  --brief <id> (--handoff <id> | --spec <path> --review <id> --round <n>)` from the frozen or
  `HEAD` copy; the elephant and the foreman paste its output. The dispatch's model and effort are
  the file's `model`/`effort` when set, else `config.reviewers.model`/`effort`, and the
  `SUPEREGM_GOLDFISH_*` overrides apply to both as today.
- **Recorded.** A reviewer's round record carries `persona: {id, sha}` for a repository or
  overridden reviewer (absent for a built-in with no override), and findings ids keep the
  `<id>-<round>-<n>` form. The Astra lane is not a goldfish and is unchanged.

SuperEGM's brainstorming skill is superpowers 7.0.0's (MIT) with two of its parts removed: the
builder check and the visual companion. Without them the brainstorm hands over a design no
builder has read and talks through every layout and diagram in prose. 1.2.1 restores both, with
7.0.0's wording, and keeps SuperEGM's additions (the brief lookup, the team, the Units section, the
`review-spec` gate). The older checklist model and a team on by default were considered and not
taken (§12).

- **The builder check.** Before the brainstorm hands the spec over, and before the SDLC workflow

  the person who must build it and reports only the questions where a wrong guess matters, ranked,

  - *Answered already*: the answer goes into the spec.
  - *Minor*: the skill settles it and marks it in the spec as its own call.
  - *Theirs*: one message to the owner, most important first.
  It then hands the spec over with the list of calls it made. Where no subagent tool exists

- **The visual companion.** The skill's `## Show, Don't Tell` and `## Visual Companion` sections,

  ends. Where the helper cannot run (Cowork), or no browser opens, the skill says so once and
  stays in text. Nothing the companion shows leaves the machine; `docs/privacy.md` says so.

## 4. The review round cap

- `review.rounds`: an integer 1–10, default **3**, registered by `review.py`. It replaces the
  constant `CAP = 5` (`skills/plan/scripts/superegm/review.py:52`) for every review kind: spec, plan,
  head and integrate. The judge panel's own `CAP = 5` (`skills/plan/scripts/superegm/judges.py:25`)
  is unchanged. A value outside 1–10 is refused by the command that reads it, naming the key.
- A spec may carry one `Rounds: <n>` line, `n` 1–10, before its first H2. `approve-spec` parses it,
  refuses any other value and a second `Rounds:` line before the first H2, each with the line, and
  freezes the value in the approval entry; with no such line it freezes the live `review.rounds`. A
  `Rounds:` line after the first H2 is prose. The frozen value governs that handoff's plan, head and
  integrate reviews. It cannot govern the spec's own review, which runs before approval under
  `review.rounds`.
- A cycle stores `cap` when it opens and keeps it; a record without one (opened under 1.2.0) reads
  as 5.
- The rest is unchanged, kind by kind: one more round per `continue` ruling; at the cap a spec, plan
  or integrate review is ruled continue, accept or stop, a head continue, stop or redesign (its
  accept stays refused, `review.py:1650`); an early accept below the cap works as in 1.2. The owner's
  `proceed` (§4.2) ends any of them at any point.

### 4.1 A narrowed panel

- Spec reviews only. The owner narrows an open spec cycle's panel the way they choose a panel
  today: by the panel the invocation carries, `config.reviewers.spec` or the invocation's
  `--reviewers` (`review_loop.md` §1 step 2). A re-typed `/superegm:review-spec` already passes that
  panel to `review open`, which resumes the open cycle and ignores it (`review.py:436-441`). In
  1.2.1 the resume compares it with the panel of the cycle's next round.
- The next round is the first round with no record yet; its current panel is the reviewers of the
  last entry of the review's `panels` list whose `from_round` is at most that round, else the
  cycle's panel (`review["reviewers"]`), so a 1.2.0 record reads as before. When the given panel,
  `astra` aside, is a proper subset of that current panel's non-`astra` reviewers and still starts
  with `design` (`FIRST`), the resume appends `{"from_round": <n>, "reviewers": <given panel + the
  cycle's Astra lane>}` to `panels` and journals the narrowing. Any other given panel (wider,
  overlapping, or equal) changes nothing, so a panel never widens; a given panel not starting with
  `design` is refused before the resume, as in 1.2 (`review.py:424-425`).
- Every resume of a spec cycle prints a second line after the id, changed or not (other kinds print
  the id alone, as in 1.2): `panel <a,b,astra> from round <n>`, the next
  round's panel, followed by `(unchanged: <reason>)` when a given panel that differs was not taken.
  The review loop dispatches that line's panel, not the one the invocation carried
  (`review_loop.md` §1 step 4 and §2; `skills/review-spec/SKILL.md:91-92`, `:110`).
- `derive_what_ran` and `review record`'s refusal (`review.py:509`) read the round's panel, so a
  dropped reviewer needs no `--not-run` record and a round of the narrowed panel can complete.
- Convergence, the cap, the stall rule and `review status` are unchanged: they count `completed`
  rounds, which a narrowed round now can be. A dropped reviewer's findings in earlier rounds keep
  their dispositions; the clean rule reads the latest round only, as in 1.2.
- Plan, head and integrate reviews keep 1.2's fixed panel: review-plan resumes a cycle without
  calling `review open` (`review_plan.py:71-84`), the wave fixes a head's panel in its run card
  (`foreman_brief.md:38`), and integrate runs one round (`skills/integrate/SKILL.md:47-64`).

### 4.2 Proceed: the owner's go-ahead

The owner can end any review, of any kind, at any round, whatever its state: not converged, highs
unsettled, below or at the cap, stalled, stopped, or with the reviewed bytes moved. The ruling
stands; nothing downstream re-checks convergence against it.

- **Who.** The owner alone, in either mode: `superegm review rule --review <id> --ruling proceed
  --owner --reason "<text>" --runcard <card>` in an interactive session, for any kind. On a head the
  owner may instead post `**Ruling:** proceed — <reason>` on the unit's issue; the wave's ruling path
  (`skills/wave/references/rulings.md`) runs the same command with `--posted <comment id>` in place
  of `--reason`, and `rule_review` checks the comment as `review settle` does (`review.py:705-713`):
  author in `config.owners`, `ruling_of` of kind `proceed`. A spec or plan pull request stays no
  place to rule (`skills/review-spec/SKILL.md:130`). `--judges` with `proceed` is refused
  (`review rule: proceed is the owner's`); no judge panel is ever asked it. `decision.py`'s
  `FORM_RES` gains `proceed{REASON}`; `RULINGS` gains `proceed`. `rule_review` reads `reason` and
  `posted` with `getattr`, since `decision.py:560` calls it with a namespace lacking them.
- **What it does.** One `rulings[]` entry `{"id": "R<n>", "ruling": "proceed", "by", "at", "reason",
  "sha256"}`, where `sha256` pins the artifact's bytes at the ruling (a head: its head sha). Every
  unsettled finding in every round of the cycle, external copies included
  (`settle_external_copies`, `review.py:644`), is settled `declined` with `by: "owner"` and
  `ruling: "R<n>"`, highs included, so they stay listed as accepted risk. Every open decision naming
  the review (a cap question, a point-5 decision) closes as answered by it. `evaluate` checks for
  the latest `proceed` ruling first, before fix-design, moved bytes, the stall and the cap
  (`review.py:880-890`): with one the review reads `converged: "by proceed R<n>"` at its latest
  recorded round (0 when none), `review status` exits 0, and its sha is the ruling's `sha256`, so
  `store_verdict`'s re-derivation after any later settle or ingest (`review.py:985-995`) keeps it. A journal line `review <id>
  proceeded by the owner: <k> findings accepted (<h> high)` records it.
- **After it.** `review record` on that cycle is refused (`review <id> proceeded by R<n>`); a fresh
  review is a new cycle, since `latest_cycle` (`review.py:371`) treats a `proceed` ruling as it
  treats `accept` and `stop` and closes the cycle. A later proceed re-pins the sha. `review ingest` still records new pull
  request threads, and `approve-spec` does not refuse a proceeded cycle's unsettled external
  findings (`approve_spec.py:190-199`); it checks the spec's bytes against the pinned sha, as it
  checks the converged round's today (`approve_spec.py:205`), so bytes changed after the ruling need
  a new proceed. `gate.converged_review` accepts `converged` starting `by proceed` before its
  `converged_round` test (`gate.py:48`) and returns the ruling's `sha256`; `review_plan.due_rows`
  (`review_plan.py:79-83`) compares a unit's body sha with that pinned sha, so a proceeded unit is
  `converged`, never `due`. On a head it writes the clean outcome as an
  accept does (`write_head_outcome`, `review.py:758`): the unit awaits the owner's merge.
- **Where it is offered.** At the cap and on a stall the review-spec and review-plan skills name
  `proceed` beside 1.2's options (`skills/review-spec/SKILL.md` steps 5 and 6;
  `skills/review-plan/SKILL.md`), and a chain wait record for either (§7.2) names it. The stall rule
  stays as 1.2's: a stall ends the invocation, and `proceed` is the way past it.
- Anti-goal 1 is unchanged: triage never defers a high. `proceed` is the owner's own ruling, not
  triage, and merging stays the owner's.

## 5. Medium triage

### 5.1 In the spec

Triage is declared by a spec's optional triage section: the first H2, outside a fenced block, whose
text is exactly `Triage`. No section: triage is off, and the 1.2 rule (a high or medium blocks until
fixed or ruled) stands. Inside the section the parser reads one `Unblock:` line and the run of `- `
lines directly after it; the run ends at the first other line, and any line outside it is prose and
ignored.

```markdown
## Triage

Unblock:
- reach: forged, later: easy
- critical: no, later: easy, verified: confirmed
```

- The fields are fixed, and every triage answers all four:
  - **reach** `external | agent | config | forged`: can an HTTP caller, an agent action, or a
    plausible configuration mistake trigger it, or only a hand-forged in-process object?
  - **later** `easy | migration | version`: easy to fix later, or does the fix need a migration or a
    new declaration version?
  - **critical** `yes | no`: on the critical path with serious ramifications?
  - **verified**: `confirmed <file:line>` (the defect is at that line) or `refuted <reason>` (the
    triager finds no defect), matched as `confirmed` or `refuted`.
- `Unblock`: patterns, numbered 1, 2, … in order, each one or more `field: value` pairs separated by
  `, ` that must all hold. A pattern applies to mediums only.
- `approve-spec` parses the section and freezes the patterns into the approval entry. It refuses,
  with the line at fault: an unknown field or value, a field repeated in one pattern, an empty
  pattern, a second `Unblock:` line, and a section with no `Unblock:` line or no pattern. The wave
  reads the frozen copy only; editing the section needs a new approval.
- The brainstorming skill offers the section and writes it with the owner. Spec review checks it:
  `structural` the grammar, `design` whether the patterns are safe for the project.

### 5.2 On a pull-request head

- **Eligible.** A panel medium of the round that is not a regression, not a `test-delta:` finding,
  not named in an `owner:` line, and not an `external:<login>` finding, in a unit whose triage the
  owner has not turned off by an undo (below). Every other finding blocks as in 1.2.
  `superegm review triage --review <id> --round <n> --eligible` prints the eligible fids.
- **Who triages.** When the frozen triage is on and the round has an eligible medium, after the head
  round is recorded and before `review post`, the foreman dispatches one `triage` goldfish (a new
  zero-context, read-only adapter, `superegm:triage`) with the four field definitions and every
  eligible medium of the round, never the patterns. Its adapter forbids reading the spec or anything
  under `docs/specs/`. It runs with the head reviewers' model and effort. It reads the code at the
  head in the review worktree and writes one line per finding, with `verified` last and taking the
  rest of the line:
  `<fid> | reach: <v> | later: <v> | critical: <v> | verified: confirmed <file:line>`, or
  `<fid> | reach: <v> | later: <v> | critical: <v> | verified: refuted <reason>`.
  The foreman records the reply verbatim with `superegm review triage --review <id> --round <n>
  --file <path> --isolation <…> --model <…> --effort <…> --runcard <card>`, as it records a
  reviewer's findings file today; it neither writes nor edits a value.
- **Refusals.** Triage off; a finding not eligible; an eligible medium with no line; an unknown
  value; a missing field; a `confirmed` whose `<file:line>` does not exist at the round's head; a
  second triage of the round. One bad line refuses the whole file, and a refused file records
  nothing. On a refusal or a failed dispatch, the foreman dispatches the triage goldfish once more;
  a second failure of either kind posts the round with those mediums blocking, as with triage off.
- **Deferred.** An eligible medium whose triage matches a pattern no longer blocks: it gets the
  disposition `deferred: triage <n>`, `n` the first matching pattern, one journal line, one row in
  `foreman/<unit>.json.followups[]` (`fid`, round, claim, triage, pattern) written when the round
  posts, by `count_posted` (`review.py:1593`, recovery included, once per fid and round), so a round
  that never posts (not run, or superseded) leaves no row, and the review comment
  `render_comment` builds lists it under **Deferred by triage**. The builder may still fix it.
- **Each round.** A medium re-reported in a later round is triaged again on the new head; a
  deferral is not carried forward. That is intended: the head changed.
- **Undo.** The owner says so in the inbox (§7.3), which posts `**Ruling:** <fid> finding upheld (fix it)` on the unit's
  issue, where every ruling reader looks (`collect.py:553`, `status.py:416-419`), naming a
  deferred medium of the unit's latest posted round. `tick_trigger` sees it as any ruling, and the

  `--decision`. Today that drops an `upheld` no open decision names (`decision.py:776-787`); in
  1.2.1 an `upheld` on a triage-deferred fid is checked as the undo first, before `decision rule`
  looks for a decision by fid (`fid_decisions`, `decision.py:816-823`), so it never binds to an older
  point-1 decision. On a fid of the unit's latest posted round it is applied there, with no new

  `disposition is None`, `review.py:871`, `:910`, `:954`), sets `undone_by: <comment id>` on the

  (`kind: undo`, `decision: null`, `fid`, `by`, `posted`, `ruling`, `posted_at` the verified
  comment's `updated_at` or `created_at`, `at`; `decision: null` keeps every reader that compares
  `r["decision"]`, `decision.py:445`, `:742`, `:746`, `:860`, false on it, and `posted_at` is what

### 5.3 An external comment reaches the builder whole

A 1.2 bug found in use. `review ingest` turns each external thread into a finding whose claim is
the thread's first line cut to 200 characters (`thread_line`, `review.py:1079`), and
`thread_severity` reads that same first line (`review.py:1108`). Devin starts every comment with a
hidden `<!-- devin-review-comment {…} -->` tag, so the stored claim was the tag alone. The
builder sees only the stored claim, and a builder account cannot read the pull request's review
comments, so in round 9 of a 1.2 build the builder got three empty findings and answered
`contested`.

1.2.1 changes both readers:

- They read the thread's root comment only. `read_threads` joins the root and every reply into
  `body` (`review.py:1045`); it also keeps the root's text as `root`, on comment and review-body threads alike, and
  `thread_line` and `thread_severity` read `thread.get("root", thread["body"])`, so a thread dict
  built by hand with `body` alone (the test fixtures) reads as before. Replies (the builder's `fixed` and `contested`, the owner, a login
  below `ingest.human_role`) never enter the bot's claim or set its severity. `body` and its other
  readers are unchanged.
- Before either reads it, the root loses every HTML comment, `<!--` to the next `-->` across
  lines, or to the end of the text when none follows (GitHub hides that too).
- The claim is the rest of the root with each run of whitespace, line breaks included, written as
  one space, then masked as today (`mask`). Up to 1,000 characters are kept; a longer claim keeps
  999 and ends with `…`, and the builder reads the thread on the pull request for the rest.
- A root with no text left gives the claim `(no text outside hidden markup)` and severity `low`.
- `thread_severity` reads the first line of the root that has text after the strip, by the same
  rules as today.
- Stored claims are rewritten. The next ingest re-reads every ingested thread still on the pull
  request through `thread_line` and rewrites each stored claim that differs, keeping its severity
  and disposition (`review.py:1350-1360`). An in-flight 1.2 unit's tag-only claims get their text
  that way; a thread GitHub no longer returns keeps its claim. A stored claim that is the
  200-character cut of the re-read claim (below) is kept as it is, so the cut survives later
  ingests.

Each finding line stays one line, so `parse_findings`, the posted round comment and the brief keep
their form. The ingest comment refuses the whole ingest over `COMMENT_LIMIT`, 65,536 bytes
(`review.py:1184`, `:1302`): at 1,000 characters a claim that is about 60 new ASCII threads in one
ingest. When the rendered comment would go over it, every claim in that ingest is cut to 200
characters with `…`, as 1.2 did, stored and posted alike, so the resume's check of the planned lines
against the posted body still holds; only beyond 1.2's own bound does it refuse. The foreman copies
each unreplied external claim into its next findings (`collect.py:628`), which `review post` scans
at 49,152 bytes, so about 45 unreplied capped claims in one round refuse that post.

## 6. Unit size and wave speed

```json
"units": {"max_body_kb": 25, "max_files": 10, "max_steps": 16}
```

- **Limits.** A unit's rendered body at most `max_body_kb` × 1024 bytes (25,600), at most
  `max_files` `Scope paths`, at most `max_steps` steps. Registered by `scan.py`; each value is a
  positive integer, `max_body_kb` at most 48 (so a body stays under 1.2's 49,152 bytes and GitHub's
  65,536-character issue body), and `max_files` at least 8 (Size M's limit). The limits are read live at each
  `scan`, never frozen. `scan` checks unfiled units only; a filed unit is never checked again. A
  lowered limit can refuse a reviewed plan's unfiled unit; the owner then re-plans (§9). The body
  limit applies to unit bodies only; `BODY_LIMIT = 49_152`
  (`skills/plan/scripts/superegm/__init__.py:65`) stays the limit of titles, the tracking issue and
  comments (`scan_text`). Size's parse-time limits (`SIZE_LIMIT`, `__init__.py:64`: S 3, M 8, L
  unlimited) are unchanged; `max_files` bounds every size at `scan`.
- **Target.** `skills/plan/references/writing_plans.md` aims each unit at one behaviour reviewable as
  one pull request of about 600 changed lines. It is guidance; no key holds it and nothing refuses on
  it.
- **The plan splits only through the spec.** Every plan unit is one row of the spec's `## Units`
  table (`skills/plan/SKILL.md:93`), so a unit over a limit needs new rows: the plan stops for
  `approve-spec --amend-units`, which the owner types, as in 1.2 (and under the chain, as a wait
  record, §7.2). A spec's rows are therefore written to fit the limits. The plan skill's splitting
  rule (`skills/plan/SKILL.md:109`) and the `proportion` reviewer read the `units.*` limits in place
  of 1.2's 24,576 bytes and eight Scope paths; the 10-step split candidate stays. `scan` refuses a
  plan with any unfiled unit over a limit, naming the unit, the limit and the value. `scan`'s
  24,576-byte warning goes (the limit replaces it); its 10-step warning stays.
- **Evidence.** The figures are the owner's, measured on the 1.0 build's filed bodies. U01 (24 KB, 8
  files, 16 steps) merged first time; U09 (94 KB, 25 files, 44 steps) took 11 launches; U02–U07
  (59–94 KB, 22–52 files, 26–60 steps) went the same way. 1.2's 48 KiB limit already refuses all of
  those; the new limits target the 25–48 KiB band, which no build has tried. The defaults are a
  judgement resting on U01 alone: its body and file count rounded up, its step count kept. The 1.2.1
  build cannot test them: it is planned under 1.2.0's limits and moves builders to Sonnet (§10).
  `units.*` raise them per project.
- **Frozen fields carried.** An `--amend-units` entry copies every frozen field (`rounds`, `triage`,
  `mode`, `instructions`) of the entry it extends, from one list in `approve_spec.py` that each unit
  adding a field extends. The `Rounds:` line and the triage section sit before `## Units`, which must be the last
  H2, so `amend_units`'s existing refusal of any change there (`approve_spec.py:278-280`, `a line
  outside ## Units changed`) already makes changing them a new approval; no new comparison.
  Every reader takes the frozen fields from the handoff's latest approval entry.
- **Approved under 1.2.0.** An approval entry without a frozen field, or a handoff with no approval
  entry (a legacy flat store's, `__init__.py:428-431`), reads: `rounds` 5, `triage`
  off, `instructions` `[]`; with no `mode`, a reader takes the live `config.mode`, as in 1.2. Such a
  handoff is not chained: the tick launches only its `wave` row, with `--leavemealone`, as 1.2's
  driver does (§7.1).

### 6.1 Shared files build in parallel

Other 1.2.1 changes already cut wave time (§1): the cap of 3 (§4) bounds redo loops like U22's 12
launches; triage (§5) ends launches made only to fix mediums; `proceed` (§4.2) ends a stuck review;
the chain (§7) starts the next step without the owner; a merged unit frees its slot (U55); Sonnet
builders (§10) may be faster per launch, unmeasured. None of them lets two units that edit one
file build at the same time. This section does.

- **Readiness.** A unit is ready when every unit it is `Blocked by` has landed. A shared-file
  predecessor no longer holds its launch. `Graph` (`__init__.py:1340-1362`) keeps two edge sets:
  `preds`, the blockers alone, which `status_pass`'s ready list (`__init__.py:2068-2069`), the
  waves and the board's waits (`report.py:147`) read; and `merge_after`, the shared-file orders. The
  cycle check runs over both, as today. `Graph` takes the handoff's approval entry, at both places
  it is built (`__init__.py:1444` and `graph.py:260`, which writes `waves` into `handoff_units.json`). With
  the rows of 1.2.1's first 25 units, blockers alone give 5 waves in place of 9.
- **What `Blocked by` must carry.** A unit that needs another's code (calls its function, extends
  its change, consumes its interface) names it in `Blocked by`, as 1.2's Consumes rule already
  requires of a producer. A shared-file order says only which of two pull requests merges first.
  The plan skill (`skills/plan/SKILL.md`) and `skills/plan/references/handoff_plan.md` say so.
  The `width:` line (`graph.py:345`) is unchanged. In the issue body's shared-file line
  (`graph.py:291-292`, `skills/plan/references/issue_template.md:68`), `after` and `before` become
  `merges after` and `merges before`, the references rendered as today, for a handoff whose
  approval carries `rounds`; so a builder does not expect the earlier unit's code in its base.
- **Merge order.** A clean head whose `merge_after` units have not all landed shows on the board
  and in the inbox as `await-merge after <unit>, …`; the inbox lists clean heads in merge order.
  The owner still merges (a non-goal to change); an out-of-order merge is not refused, and the
  conflict rule below handles what it breaks.
- **Conflict.** `read_pr` (`__init__.py:1938-1941`) also reads `mergeable` (GitHub's `MERGEABLE`,
  `CONFLICTING` or `UNKNOWN`); `UNKNOWN` is read again on the next pass. An open pull request whose
  head is `CONFLICTING` gives its unit the view status `conflict`, which is a view change for
  `tick_trigger` and `wait_events`; `unit_view` sets it after the CI column is read from the status
  (`status.py:385-386`), so the CI value is kept. What happens next depends on the unit's state:
  - **Its head is clean or proceeded, awaiting merge.** The unit is ready again: `launch`'s `again`
    set (`launch.py:80-81`) takes it when no launch of the unit is running and none was recorded
    since its latest round, it waits for a slot like any ready unit, and `launch-builder` starts a
    **merge round** at the pull request's tip.
  - **Its latest round asks for fixes.** No extra launch: the next fix round's orders also carry
    the merge orders, and that round counts toward the cap as any fix round does.
  - **A launch is running, or the unit waits on the owner** (a cap question, a stop, any open
    decision). Nothing is launched; the board and the inbox show `conflict` beside the unit, and the
    rule is applied again once the launch ends or the owner rules.

  `launch-builder` adds the merge orders when the unit's saved pull request (`status.py:379`) reads
  `CONFLICTING`, and writes `merge: "alone"` on the launch entry when the foreman's latest round is
  clean or proceeded, `merge: "with-fix"` otherwise. The merge orders: merge `refs/superegm/base` into the branch (a merge commit; no rebase, no force
  push), resolve each conflict keeping both units' intent, run the unit's tests, push. On a launch
  that carries them, `allowed_tools()` (`launch_builder.py:383`) also admits `Bash(git merge:*)`.
  The brief names the conflicting paths (`git merge-tree --write-tree` of the head and the base)
  and the landed units whose scope holds them. The rule is the same whatever caused the conflict, a
  unit of another handoff included.
- **The review after a merge round.** The head moved, so the foreman reviews it. The foreman entry
  of a round whose `launch` names an entry with `merge: "alone"` carries `merge: true`, and that round does not
  count toward the cap (§4): a unit is not stopped by a conflict it did not cause. A merge round
  follows only a conflict on a clean head, so their number is bounded by the units that land on the
  base. After a head the owner proceeded (§4.2), the merge round's review is a new review cycle,
  as §4.2 says of any fresh review.
- **No conflict, no relaunch.** A clean head that stays `MERGEABLE` after a predecessor lands is
  neither relaunched nor reviewed again. Where the base's ruleset requires branches to be up to
  date, GitHub's "Update branch" is the owner's, as today.
- **Handoffs approved under 1.2.0.** An approval entry without the `rounds` field (§6, "Approved
  under 1.2.0") keeps 1.2's rule: `preds` holds the shared-file orders too, and no merge round runs;
  a `conflict` view only shows on the board and in the inbox, and the owner resolves it as in 1.2.
  `--amend-units` never adds a field the extended entry lacks, so such a handoff stays serial. A
  plan written for serial units is never run in parallel behind its author's back.

### 6.2 A fix round resumes the builder's session

- **When.** A launch of round 2 or later (a fix after a review, a merge round, a relaunch after an
  ingest) resumes the unit's previous launch when all of these hold: that launch recorded a
  `session_id` and a result (its result file parses to `type: "result"` with `is_error` false); its transcript is found as `traces.subagent_calls` finds it
  (`traces.py:125`, `projects/*/<id>.jsonl` under the builder profile); and the new launch's model,
  effort and builder profile equal that launch's (a tier change starts fresh). The launch entry
  gains `profile_dir` for that check. Otherwise the launch
  is fresh, as in 1.2. Only the immediately previous launch is looked at, never an older one.
- **How.** `launch-builder` launches with `--resume <id> --fork-session --session-id <new uuid>`
  from the new round's worktree (`launch_builder.py:501`). The platform finds a session by its id
  whatever the working directory, the earlier transcript is left as it was, and the new session's
  transcript and id are the new worktree's, so `record-launch` and `traces` read it as today
  (`launch_builder.py:131`). (Checked on 2026-11-13: a `-p` session started in one directory and
  resumed this way from another kept its context, and its new transcript was written under the
  second directory's project.) The flags are built in U60's one function, so the probe and the
  launch stay the same shape. A forked transcript repeats the earlier history, so a resumed
  launch counts only tool-use ids not in the resumed session's transcript. Every reader counts
  through one helper, `traces.launch_subagent_calls(profile_dir, entry)` (U49: `record-launch`'s
  finish, `interrupt --launch` and the reboot proof call it in place of `traces.subagent_calls`,
  `traces.py:117`); U63 changes only that helper, to read the entry's `resumed_from`.
- **The prompt.** A resumed launch's prompt is `prompt_text`'s output (`launch_builder.py:455`)
  unchanged: the orders name this round's paths (its `gh` shim, `launch_builder.py:96-97`), so they
  are sent again. It opens with one line naming the round's worktree and saying that paths from
  earlier rounds are stale.
- **The record.** The launch entry carries `resumed_from: <id>` or `fresh: <reason>` (`first
  round`, `no result`, `no transcript`, `model changed`, the last covering model, effort or
  profile). A resumed launch that ends with no result and no commit of its own is a **failed
  resume**: `record-launch`'s finish (`record_launch.py:113-117`) keeps the status `exited <code>`
  (so the report, `foreman.py:123` and every terminal reader are unchanged), adds
  `failed_resume: true` to the entry and prints `failed resume`; the wave skill's step 3 (`skills/wave/SKILL.md:123-127`) runs
  `launch-builder` for the next round at once, which is fresh by the rule above; and `no_commit`
  (`decision.py:329-359`) returns false for an entry with `failed_resume`, so it is never decision point 3's launch without a commit
  (`skills/wave/references/decision_points.md:79-83`).
- **What it buys.** The session keeps what it read of the code and the plan and what it tried, so a
  fix round does not cold-start for about 36 minutes. Unmeasured: the 1.2.1-built release's launch
  records, with `resumed_from` and each launch's duration, will show it.
- **Isolation.** A resumed session sees only what the same unit's builder already saw, its own
  earlier transcript; 1.2 §3's closed list (anti-goal 3, as §2.1 widens it) is unchanged.

### 6.3 Evidence before the push

The 1.2 build's 84 launches were mostly fix rounds after a pull-request-head review, about 36
minutes each. Its 17 head cycles recorded 131 high and 334 medium findings. Some were mechanical
(a changed path outside the unit's Scope paths, the test-first order, a DoD output missing), and
most were logic and edge cases the head panel found by reading the diff. The builder could find
both kinds before it pushes, inside the session that already holds the context.

- **The check.** A new helper subcommand, `superegm precheck --handoff <id> --unit <unit>
  --pr-body <file>`, run by the builder in its worktree, runs the foreman's checks that need only
  the worktree, through one function in `collect.py` that `collect` and `precheck` both call: every
  DoD item's fenced output and `Run` command (`dod_runs`, `:101`, its output in a temporary
  directory), the test delta (`:172`), the scope check (`:578`) and the test-first order
  (`tdd_order.check`, `:582`). Where `collect` reads the dispatch entry and the pull request,
  `precheck` reads the merge base as `git merge-base refs/superegm/base HEAD`, the PR body from
  `--pr-body` (the body file the orders have the builder write: on round 1 before
  `gh pr create --draft --body-file`, later for `gh pr edit --body-file`),
  and, from round 2, the unit's posted rounds, recorded rulings and issue comments from the store
  read-only, as `collect` reads them (`collect.py:352-381`), so a widened scope, an allowed
  test-delta entry, a ruled tdd-order failure or an allowed `dod-<n>` is not reported again. It
  prints one line per finding in the foreman's grammar and exits 0 when there is none. It writes
  nothing to the store or the worktree (`WRITES = frozenset()`) and needs no CI.
- **The self-review.** When `precheck` passes, the builder reviews its own diff against the
  merge base, in its own session, against what the head panel reviews against: the issue body's
  definition of done and its Scope paths (`foreman_brief.md:38`, `agents/change-review.md`). It
  fixes each high or medium it finds or answers it in the PR body as `**Precheck reply <n>:**
  contested <reason>`. No subagent is called: a builder profile holds no plugins and no `Agent`
  unless the owner adds them (U27), and the precheck does not change that.
- **Retry and the checkpoint.** After a fix, the builder runs `precheck` again, up to
  `builder.precheck_attempts` runs (default 3) per launch. Each commit is a checkpoint: the best
  checkpoint is the commit with the fewest `precheck` findings so far, never one older than the
  pull request's tip. When a fix leaves more findings than the best checkpoint, the builder resets
  to it (`git reset --hard <sha>`). When the attempts run out, it pushes the best checkpoint with
  its open findings listed. A precheck never holds a push back for good.
- **The evidence.** The PR body gains a `**Precheck:**` block with each run's exit and findings,
  and the self-review's findings with their replies. The foreman reads it as evidence and still runs
  every check itself. The head review is unchanged: the precheck only moves findings earlier.
- **The orders and the launch.** `builder_orders.md` gains the step before every push (rounds 1
  and later, a merge round included). `launch-builder` writes a `superegm-precheck` script into
  `launches/<unit>_r<n>_bin/` on every launch, the machine-account path included (today that
  directory and `superegm-token` exist only on the App path, `launch_builder.py:123-124`), and
  refuses whitespace in `artifacts_dir` on every launch, not only the App path (`:88-92`). The
  script quotes the interpreter and the helper inside it (`launch_builder.py:195-200`), since the
  helper's path can hold whitespace that an allow-list prefix cannot; the orders name it as
  `{{precheck}}` and run it with a Bash timeout of 600000 ms, since its DoD runs are bounded by
  `foreman.run_timeout_minutes` (up to 9, `collect.py:30-34`) and the Bash tool otherwise stops a
  call at 2 minutes. `allowed_tools()` gains
  `Bash(<bin>/superegm-precheck:*)` and `Bash(git reset --hard:*)`.
- **What it buys.** Unmeasured. The 1.2.1 build's head-round records, compared with 1.2's per
  unit (findings per round, rounds per unit), will show it. The self-review costs part of the
  same session, against a 36-minute relaunch.

### 6.4 A stronger model on a later round

- **When.** A unit's launch moves to the `difficult` tier's pair (`tier_pair`, `__init__.py:1086`)
  once the unit's current head cycle has `builder.escalate_after` posted rounds (default 2; 0
  turns it off) whose outcome is `revise` or `cap-reached` (`write_head_outcome`, `review.py:758`;
  `write_owner_outcome`, `:774`), or whose outcome is `contested:` while the round also holds an
  open blocking finding that is not waiting on a ruling: not a builder contest without a ruling,
  not a `test-delta:` finding and not an unruled point-1 finding (§6.5). `launch_builder` reads
  each such round with `review.load_round` to test this. These rounds do not count: a merge round (`merge: true`, §6.1),
  a round collected as `not run (...)` (`collect.py:342`), an `owner: <ids>` outcome waiting on a
  ruling, and rounds of a cycle a `proceed` closed. None of them says the model fell short. It is
  upward only and stays for the rest of the unit's rounds: a launch also escalates when any earlier
  launch entry of the unit carries `escalated: true`, so a new cycle after a `proceed` does not
  drop back. A unit already planned `difficult`, or
  a config with no `difficult` row, does not change. With the shipped defaults `doable` and
  `difficult` share a model and differ in effort; the gain in model needs a `difficult` row such
  as this repository's (§10).
- **How.** `launch-builder` picks the pair after `unit_tier` (`launch_builder.py:82-83`). The
  launch entry records `tier: difficult` and `escalated: true`, so the change of model starts a
  fresh session (§6.2, `fresh: model changed`). The fix brief is unchanged.
- **Why per unit, not per launch.** One failing round is the normal fix round, and resuming is
  cheaper there (§6.2). Two failing rounds do not show whether the model or the brief is short:
  the 1.2 build's 63 review-driven relaunches followed findings of every kind. Escalation is a
  cheap try, not a diagnosis. §6.5 is the change aimed at the brief.


### 6.5 How SuperEGM plans

The 1.2 build and the 1.2.1 plan review show where plan detail costs more than it buys. The 1.2.1
plan reached 38 units and 417 KB with 140 code cites. Three review rounds produced 187 findings
(64 high): 127 were about tests, asserts or fixtures, and 98 cited a `.py` line. The highs did not
converge (37, 13, 14), because round-3 highs mostly came from round-2 fixes to pinned test bodies.
The 1.2 build had 69 builder relaunches: 6 followed a non-review blocker and 63 followed a review.
The 1.2 foreman's 55 findings were 34 contested carry-overs, 16 changed paths outside Scope, 4
test-order findings and 1 unruled deviation. These counts say what triggered each relaunch. They do
not say what each relaunch cost or what this section saves. Neither is claimed.

The detail also caught real defects: U45's mode order, U37's missing dependency on U36, and the
1.2 heads where a plan-prescribed ruling string failed `parse_form`. So this section keeps the
contracts and acceptance values pinned, and leaves to the builder what only the code can settle.

**What a unit pins.** These go in the body, and the builder may not change them on its own:
- The spec range, Scope, Blocked by, the Produces and Consumes lines, and the DoD command.
- Interfaces: every name and signature another unit consumes, with the return shape where a
  consumer depends on it.
- The acceptance values: the spec's exact inputs and outputs, and the refusal and error wording
  the spec fixes.
- Security, money, atomicity, lock order and compatibility rules the spec states.
- The owner's decisions.

**What a Step holds.** The Step grammar stays as 1.2 has it: a Write line naming the test and its
file, a fence, and an Implement line. `body.py`, `launch_builder.py`, `tdd_order.py` and
`collect.py` therefore read old and new plans the same way.
- The fence holds the test's name, one comment line saying the behaviour, and the assertions with
  the spec's exact values. When the spec gives several cases, it holds a parametrize table copied
  from the spec.
- The fence names an exception class, or any other name, only when the public or shared contract
  requires that name. It never names a fixture, conftest attribute, private helper or monkeypatch
  target. Those belong to the builder.
- The Implement line names the file and the behaviour. It names a signature only for a public or
  shared contract that is not already a Produces line.
- A code cite (`path:line`) is evidence, never a requirement. `scan` refuses a `:<line>` cite on
  an Interfaces or Produces line, because a consumer would bind to a line that moves. It warns,
  but does not refuse, when a body has more than ten cites into its own Scope paths. The
  byte-size and fenced-share limits do not change.

`skills/plan/references/handoff_plan.md` §3, `writing_plans.md` and the plan skill say this, and drop
their instructions to check fixtures and current line numbers. The handoff standard's Q9 counts a
Step as complete when it has the test name, the behaviour and the expected values. It is not
incomplete for leaving fixtures, arrange code, helpers or private exception types to the builder.
Under Q11, a fence that names unnecessary private or harness detail is a proportion finding
(medium). Names the public or shared contract requires are exempt.

**Readiness.** The readiness reviewer asks whether a builder who reads the code can implement the
unit without making a product, security or cross-unit contract decision. It no longer asks whether
the builder knows exactly what to code, where and how.
- An undefined API signature is a gap only for a shared contract.
- Fixture names, harness wiring, private names and line numbers are out of the reviewer's remit.
- Every finding names the requirement it breaks (a spec section, an Interfaces line or an
  acceptance value) and a failure scenario. A finding without both is low.
- A blocking question is high only when its failure scenario holds; the handoff standard's
  severity paragraph says so.
- No finding is downgraded for its words: a finding that cites a line or a fixture can still be a
  real defect.
- `agents/readiness.md` serves both spec and plan reviews. The new remit is a paragraph that
  applies when it reviews a plan; a spec review keeps today's remit.
- The proportion reviewer and the review-plan brief use the same remit.

**The seam and deviations.** Before Step 1, the builder confirms that the code matches what the
unit's Interfaces and Consumes lines expect. It posts one comment on the issue, `**Seam:** ok` or
`**Seam:** <what differs>`.
- On a mismatch in a contract, it also posts a separate
  `**Deviation (interface):** <Uxx> <name>: <what differs>` comment, naming the unit that produces
  the contract and the name in that unit's Interfaces or Produces lines. That deviation blocks and
  is the one that is ruled; the Seam comment is evidence only.
- `launch_builder.PROMPT_COMMENT_RE` and `prepare.AGED` gain `**Seam:**`, and `prompt_text`'s
  `keep` keeps a Seam comment whose author is the builder's login (`launch.builder_login`), as it
  keeps an owner's ruling. So the prompt carries the builder's own Seam comment from round 2 on,
  whatever its age.

Deviation kinds are defined by their effect:
- `interface`: a shared contract changes, or a predecessor's Produces line is missing. It blocks.
- `dod`: an acceptance value or the DoD evidence weakens. It blocks.
- `spec`: the spec itself is wrong or impossible, with the spec line and the evidence (§7.6). It blocks.
- `test`: coverage weakens, not just the test's layout. It blocks.
- `other`: an equivalent adaptation, such as a different fixture, helper or file layout inside
  Scope. The builder continues.

An equivalent adaptation does not waive a check the helper enforces on paths or names (Scope,
test-delta, TDD order). So the plan's rules also say that a unit's Scope lists the conftest and
fixture files its tests may touch (for example `tests/conftest.py`), since the builder now chooses
the fixtures.

**`amend`.** The owner can rule `**Ruling:** (<comment-id>) amend — <the Interfaces line as it now
reads>`, where `<comment-id>` is an `interface` deviation's comment. The amended contract is the
`<Uxx> <name>` that deviation names. `decision rule` refuses the ruling when that name is not in
Uxx's Interfaces lines, or when the comment holds more than one deviation heading or any heading
that is not `interface`. An ordinary option ruling on a comment with several headings still
settles them all, as today.
- `amend` is an option of every interface deviation's decision, so `decision.offered` accepts it
  and `collect` counts the deviation ruled, as it does any option ruling on that comment.
- It is the owner's alone. `decision.check_ruling` refuses it when the ruling's author starts
  `judges:`, as it refuses `<fid> allowed` today, and `judges.decision_question` leaves it out of
  the options the judges see.
- `amend` restates only an Interfaces line, including its return shape. It never changes an
  acceptance value, a test name or path, an implementation path, Scope, Blocked by, the DoD
  command, the approved spec or a dependency's identity. Those keep their existing rulings:
  `<fid> finding overruled` for a path outside Scope, `test-delta … allowed`, the `dod` and `test`
  deviations, and test order.
- `decision rule` records, as the file `amendments/<comment id>.json` in the handoff's directory
  written before the ruling itself is saved (the store's `file` kind, so `decision.py`'s `WRITES` gains `file`; idempotent on a rerun after a crash;
  `launch_builder.amendments_for` reads the directory and, for a handoff from 1.2.1-pre.1, the legacy `amendments[]` of `state.json` too), every ruling that
  changes an interface contract: the producing unit and name the deviation names, the text, the
  ruling's comment id and `at`, the ruling's record time. For `amend` the text is the owner's
  line. For an option ruling, the owner's or the judges' in leavemealone, it is the chosen
  option's text. A ruling that leaves the contract as it was (`stop the unit`, one more round, a
  relaunch) records no amendment. `launch_builder` adds to a unit's prompt every
  recorded amendment for its own Interfaces and for every name its Consumes lines list. So a
  consumer filed later gets it on its first launch. `judges.fan_out` still copies an interface
  question to the consumers already filed, as 1.2 §13.2 says.
- The filed body's bytes and its receipts never change. The unit's operative brief is the body
  plus its authorised rulings and the amendments that apply to it, and the builder and the
  foreman read all of them. The foreman holds a head to an amendment only when the head comes
  from a launch whose `started_at` is after the amendment's `at`. An earlier head is reviewed against
  the brief its launch had.

**The foreman.** The foreman reviews a head against spec behaviour and the unit's contract:
Interfaces, Scope, acceptance values, rulings and amendments. It does not review against a Step's
wording.
- A finding is kept for its demonstrated consequence: a changed behaviour that regresses, an unmet
  unit or spec contract, or a security effect. Its location never decides whether it is kept.
  Reviewers still trace the readers and writers of what the head changed, beyond the files it
  edits.
- Every finding keeps the route it has today. A changed path outside Scope is a point-1 finding:
  `overruled` widens Scope, and `upheld` means the builder reverts the path, which `collect`
  raises again while the path is still changed on a later head. A contested finding goes to
  decide, and in leavemealone to the judges, as 1.2 §13.2 says.
- What changes is that a finding waiting on a ruling is posted once. Two kinds are carried, each
  from the previous round only:
  - A builder contest with no ruling yet, on a finding `collect` does not derive. `collect`
    re-records the finding in the new round under the same id with `carried_from: <round>`,
    copies its `builder_contested` entry, and leaves it out of the comment it posts.
  - A finding `collect` derives (scope, test-delta, tdd-order, `dod`, an unruled deviation, an
    unreplied comment, CI), whether or not it waits on a ruling. It is derived again on every
    head and reuses the previous round's id with `carried_from` only while its check still fails
    with the same claim and location; it is not posted again. One that no longer fails is not
    carried, even unruled, so a reverted out-of-Scope path needs no ruling. Its record in the
    earlier round stays as it was.
- A fixable panel finding is not carried. If it still holds on the new head, the panel raises it
  again.
- A builder's `contested` reply counts when its fid is open in the previous posted round's
  record, carried copies included. `collect` (`:600`) and `timely` (`:633`) stop reading the
  round from the id; the author, time, CI and already-ruled checks stay.
- `review.evaluate` counts a carried finding against convergence like any other. A ruling settles
  it from the round it lands in, and a carried finding whose ruling exists is not carried again.
  `review settle --finding` settles it in the latest round that holds it (`locate_finding` changes
  to search newest first).
- The round's outcome word follows today's order (`outcome_word`): `contested:` first, then
  `owner:` when every open blocking finding is the owner's alone, otherwise `revise`.
  `contested_fids` reads the copied `builder_contested` entries, and `owner_only_fids` counts a
  carried owner-only finding as it counts a new one. So a round whose only open findings are
  carried and waiting on a ruling is `contested:` or `owner:`, and §6.4 does not count it. A round
  that also holds a blocking finding not waiting on a ruling (§6.4) keeps the word the order gives it, so a contest still
  goes to its decision. §6.4 counts that round when its word is `revise`, or `contested:` with
  such a finding.

**Measuring it.** `launch_builder` writes each launch's `reason` when it starts:
- `first`: the unit's first launch.
- `review`: after a posted round, whatever its word, including a relaunch after a ruling on a
  `contested:` or `owner:` round.
- `blocked`: after a ruling on a BLOCKED or NEEDS_CONTEXT status.
- `merge`: a merge round (§6.1), written by the merge round's launch (U62).
- `harness`: every other launch: a fresh launch after a failed resume (§6.2), a stall or a setup
  failure. `launch_reason` returns it when no other reason applies.

`record_launch` adds `duration_ms` from the result file (`result_usage`). The next build can then
compare relaunch counts and builder time per unit with 1.2's.

- **Amendments.** `launch_builder.amendments_for` keeps an entry whose `unit` is the launching unit,

### 7.2 Decisions under the chain

The decision points are 1.2 §13.2's four (point 1 a contested or declined high or medium; point 2 a
review at its cap; point 3 an open question, a blocked builder or a launch that ended without a
head; point 4 the owner's approval of a converged spec, and file's Create, in normal mode) and
1.2 §17 U35's accept below the cap, called point 5 here.

- **Before filing (plan, review-plan, file).** A headless phase cannot ask. Where it would ask the
  owner in normal mode (the plan's point 3, review-plan's per-round accept question, a contested
  high or medium, a review at its cap, a stall) or must stop in either mode (a split needing
  `--amend-units`, or a filing's verification problems to fix on GitHub, §7.1), it runs `superegm next wait --phase <phase> --question <text> [--review <id>] --runcard <card>` (the review cap, stall and contest waits pass `--review`; a re-ask keeps the stored value),
  which writes the record `chain/<handoff>.json` (`phase`, `question`, `since`, and `review` with the review id for a wait a review raised, kept through a re-ask) and closes the card
  it is given, and exits. Under the driver, review-plan's start question is not asked: the chain
  chose to start. A driven normal-mode file runs its gate and dry run, then stops at Create with `next wait --phase
  file`, its question naming the issue and link counts, `handoff_dryrun.md` and that file's sha256
  (§7.3). The inbox and the notify hook carry
  the question.
- **The owner's answer.** The owner answers in the inbox (§7.3), which writes the answer into the
  wait record; the next tick runs the phase headless and the skill records it as below. The owner
  may still type the phase's skill in an interactive session. Finding a
  wait record, the skill opens its card with `runcard open --mode normal` whatever the frozen mode,
  asks the owner the record's question directly, with no new panel, records the answer as a
  normal-mode run does (`review settle --owner`, `decide` by the owner) and runs the phase on.
  `decide` stores its card's mode on the decision (`decide.py:50`), and `scan`'s ruler check
  (`scan.py:129-133`) refuses an owner's answer only when the decision's stored mode is
  `leavemealone` (a decision without one reads the frozen mode, else `config.mode`);
  `review settle` already reads the card's mode (`review.py:673-677`). While its card is open the tick skips the handoff. At its end the
  session runs `superegm next clear`, which removes the record only on confirmed progress: `next`, read without it, prints a later phase or the unit withdrawn, or a ruling resolves the wait; a `waiting` row (a transient `waiting fetch` among them) keeps it. Otherwise the record stays: an unanswered record keeps the
  tick skipping the handoff, and an answered one is written again with its `answer`, `by` and `at`
  removed, a new `since` (the rewrite's time, as `next wait`'s re-ask) and the question `stopped at <phase> by <by>: answer to go on`, so an answer such as file's
  Stop or Change the plan is asked again, never relaunched in a loop.
- **After filing (wave).** Normal mode: the headless wave opens points 1, 2, 3 and 5 as `decision
  open` records (as the leavemealone wave does) instead of asking; that unit is `pending a decision`

  (`skills/wave/SKILL.md:51-54`), since `status.wait_events` has already acknowledged every event in
  the batch (`status.py:116-136`) and an early exit would lose another unit's exit; the owner answers in
  the inbox (§7.3), which posts the `**Ruling:**` comment the next tick's `tick_trigger` sees, or
  posts that comment on GitHub. Point 4 does
  not arise after filing.
- **Leavemealone** routes to the judges only what 1.2 routes to them: before filing, the panel rules
  as the plan and review-plan skills do today, and file creates; after filing, the wave as today.
  Owner-only outcomes (a `test-delta:` finding, an `owner:` line, a split needing `--amend-units`)
  and every question a panel leaves unconverged become a wait record or an open decision, never a
  new panel on the next tick.
- The run cards, journal lines and records are the same in both modes; only who answers differs.
- Merging stays the owner's. Clean heads wait for the owner's merge as in 1.2; the inbox merges on
  the owner's word (§7.3).
- A `proceed` (§4.2) answers any wait record or decision that names a review: `next` reads the review

### 7.3 The owner answers in words

The inbox becomes where the owner answers, not only where they look. Today every item prints the
text to paste (`skills/inbox/SKILL.md:26`): a `**Ruling:**` form to post on GitHub, or a command to
type. In 1.2.1 the inbox asks the owner in plain words and does the rest.

- **Who may answer.** The inbox runs in the owner's interactive session. Before it acts on an
  answer it reads `gh api user --jq .login` and refuses unless the login is in `config.owners`;
  every comment it posts is posted with that login, so every ruling reader (`collect.py:553`,
  `status.py:416-419`) sees an owner's comment exactly as if typed. The inbox opens no run card
  and runs no recorder, except `approve-spec --void` (which opens its own card, U53) and `driver
  kick`: it posts the owner's comment or writes the answer into the wait record,
  and the phase session that holds the card records it, as for a comment the owner typed.
- **The pick.** The agent usually knows the right answer, so every question opens with one
  recommended option and a one-line reason ("I'd rule (b): the builder's test covers it. Rule (b)?"),
  and the owner's "yes" takes it. The inbox session picks it from the question and its evidence
  (the round's `comments.md` and diff, the builder's comment, and `judges/<panel>/verdict.json`'s
  split where a panel ran); the owner may answer anything else in words instead.
- **In leavemealone.** The judges already settle each ruling and `decision rule … --by
  judges:<panel>` posts it (`skills/wave/references/leavemealone_mode.md`), so the inbox has
  nothing to ask about those. It lists only what stays the owner's: a question the panel did not
  converge on, a `redesign`, an `owner:` or test-delta finding and a `kill_failed` unit
  (`decision_points.md:17-19`, `:27`; `inbox.py:130-137`, `:154`), a merge, and a wait before
  filing that the frozen mode leaves to the owner, each with its pick.
- **A ruling after filing** (decision points 1, 2, 3, 5, a contested finding, a head's cap or
  stall, `proceed` §4.2, an undo §5.2). The inbox shows the question, its options and the pick,
  takes the owner's answer in words, maps it to an option and reads the mapped ruling back once ("rule (b) —
  <reason>?"). On yes it posts the `**Ruling:**` comment in the form
  `skills/wave/references/rulings.md` reads, with `gh issue comment <n> -R <repo> --body-file
  <file>` on the issue that reader looks at, and kicks (§7.4). The next wave session sees it
  through `status.tick_trigger` and records it with `--posted`, as in 1.2.
- **A wait before filing.** `superegm next answer --handoff <id> --answer <text>` (new, in
  `phase.py`) reads the login itself (`gh api user --jq .login`, `GH_HOST` pinned as
  `core.gh_host` does), writes `answer`, `by` and `at` into `chain/<handoff>.json`, and refuses
  when there is no record, when the record already holds an answer, or when the login is not in
  `config.owners`. `next` then prints the record's phase (§7.1's table, after the push and checkout
  rows) and the tick runs it headless. The driven skill, finding an answered record, opens its card
  with `runcard open --mode normal` exactly as the owner's typed run does, records the answer as
  the owner's (`review settle --owner`, `decide --answer`, an `accept`, `proceed` or `stop`
  ruling) with the record's `by` and `at` in the note, and runs `superegm next clear` at its end.
  An answer the skill cannot map to one of the question's options writes the record again, with
  `next wait`, with a new `question` naming what it could not map, and no `answer`; the unmapped
  answer stays in `waits/` (§7.5). A driven review-plan that stops
  writes a wait record as for a stall (§7.2), so `proceed` on a stopped plan review goes the same
  way.
- **File's Create in normal mode** (`waiting file: Create these <n> issues and <m> links …`). The
  driven file run has made its dry run and asked Create, Change the plan or Stop as today
  (`skills/file/SKILL.md:104`); the inbox shows `handoff_dryrun.md` with the question and answers
  it like any wait before filing. Launched again on the answered record, the file run makes the dry
  run again and creates only when its sha256 is the one in the question; otherwise it asks again.
- **Nothing left to plan** (`waiting plan: no unit left to plan`). The inbox asks "void the
  approval?" and on yes runs `superegm approve-spec --void --spec <spec>`, which, typed without
  `--runcard`, opens and closes its own `approve-spec` card (U53).
- **A spec review or integrate ruling.** The owner is in that skill's interactive session, which
  asks in words as in 1.2; the inbox lists it as "answer in your `<skill>` session", never as a
  command.
- **Merging.** For a merge-ready pull request the inbox asks "merge <PR>?" and, on yes, runs `gh pr
  merge <n> -R <repo> --<method> --match-head-commit <sha>`, `<repo>` the item's store's repository

Right before each `gh pr merge`, the ordinary one and the `--admin` one alike, it reads the unit's state again: the unit not stopped, no respec mark, no pending decision, no live launch, and the head's review verdict clean or proceeded; when any of these changed since the question, it withdraws the offer, journals `merge withdrawn <PR> <reason>` and merges nothing.

  <root>`, `<root>` the item's store's registered root (§7.4).
- **The rule.** No skill asks the owner to post a comment or to paste a command. `using-superegm`

### 7.4 The chain by default and the kick

- **On at the owner's yes in the SDLC workflow step.** The SDLC workflow step (§3.4) offers the chain once doctor passes,
  sets `driver.chain` to `true` and commits it when the owner agrees; the env file, `register` and
  `driver install` then run from review-spec's approval step (§3.4 area 7), with a typed
  `/superegm:driver` as the fallback; `init`
  writes no `driver.chain`, so an owner who declines has the chain off. An absent key reads `false`, so a 1.2 store does not start running
  unattended phases on upgrade; `doctor`'s `chain: off` line shows it.
- **The interval.** `driver install`'s default interval drops from 15 minutes to 5
  (`driver.py:79`, `:91`). An installed driver keeps its interval until `driver install` runs again.
- **The kick.** `superegm driver kick --root <root>` runs one store's tick now: `tick_store`
  (`driver.py:276`) for the registered entry whose root is `<root>`, with `~/.superegm/env` applied
  and restored around it exactly as `tick()` does (`driver.py:322-326`). It does nothing for an
  unregistered root, when the driver is not installed (`driver.py:36-44`'s plist or unit file), or
  when that store's `driver.chain` is not `true`: a store registered without `driver install` never
  starts an unattended session from a kick, and a chain-off store keeps 1.2's interval-only driver.
  Like `tick` and `status`, `kick` runs without a run card (`driver.run` handles it before
  `require_runcard`, `driver.py:85-89`).
- **The lock.** `tick` and `kick` share a per-store lock (`fcntl.flock` on `tick/lock`,
  non-blocking). Each first writes `tick/again`, then tries the lock. One that cannot take it skips
  that store (`tick` goes on to the next store) and leaves `tick/again`. The holder removes
  `tick/again` before each `tick_store` and, after it releases, takes the lock again while
  `tick/again` exists. So two kicks never launch twice, and a kick during a tick is never lost.
- **Who kicks.** The builder's wrapper (`launch_builder.py:540-575`), after it writes `.exit`; the
  driver's phase wrapper (`tick_command`, `driver.py:152-159`), after its session ends; and the
  inbox after an answer or a merge (§7.3). Every kick runs as `env -i HOME="$HOME" PATH="$PATH"`
  with the helper's interpreter and path quoted, so no builder token, profile variable or the
  owner's session environment reaches the tick; the kick applies the env file itself. A kick's
  failure is ignored and logged to the launch's log: the next interval still ticks.
- **What still waits for the interval.** A CI result, a comment posted on GitHub by hand and a
  merge done outside the inbox: GitHub sends no event the driver listens for.

### 7.5 The post-mortem

Once a handoff's build is done the store already holds every launch, round, finding, ruling,
decision and wait. 1.2.1 turns it into one report the owner reads.

- **The command.** `superegm postmortem --handoff <id>` writes `<handoff dir>/postmortem.md` and
  prints its path. It reads the store only, needs no run card and writes nothing else
  (`WRITES = frozenset(['file'])`, as `scan`'s; the report is a derived file); its one other
  output is `retro.md`, written only by the `--retro <file>` form (§7.7). It runs on an open handoff
  too, the header then saying `in progress`.
- **What it holds.** Fixed sections, numbers only, each a table:
  1. *Header*: handoff, spec, approval commit, opened and closed times, mode, units, waves.
  2. *Units*: per unit, tier, model(s) used, launches (fresh, resumed, failed resume, escalated,
     merge rounds), head review rounds, findings by severity, wall time from first launch to merge,
     and cost and tokens where launches recorded them.
  3. *Reviewers*: per reviewer and kind (spec, plan, head, integrate), findings raised by severity
     and how they ended (fixed, overruled, allowed, deferred by triage, regressions), and rounds
     where it raised nothing.
  4. *Owner time*: every decision and wait record, what asked it, how long it waited, who answered
     (owner, judges, default); every `proceed` and cap stop. A wait record is rewritten and removed
     by `next clear` (§7.2), so its history comes from the journal, one line per revision:
     `next wait` journals `next wait <handoff> <phase> since <at> source <source> question <sha>`
     (the question text's sha256, the text itself kept as `waits/<phase>-<at>.md` in the handoff's
     directory, the store's `file` kind), a re-ask (§7.3) journals
     `next reask <handoff> <phase> at <at> question <sha>` with its text kept the same way;
     `next answer` (§7.3) keeps the answer's words as `waits/<phase>-<since>.answer.md`, keyed by
     the wait's `since` so it pairs with its question, before any re-ask replaces the record, and
     beside it writes `waits/<phase>-<since>.answer.json` (`{by, at, sha}`, the answer's sha256), which no re-ask or `next clear` rewrites, so the post-mortem reads who answered and when from it and counts the wait up to that `at`; `next clear` journals `next clear <handoff> <phase> answered by <by> at <at> answer <sha>`.
     A re-ask is the skill's `next wait` on a phase whose record holds an answer; `next wait`
     journals it as `next reask`, keeps the new question as `waits/<phase>-<at>.md` and sets the
     record's `since` to that `at`, so each question pairs with its own answer file; the
     post-mortem sums the waiting across a phase's journal lines. These
     files are the store's `file` kind, so `phase.py`'s `WRITES` gains `file`.
  5. *Plan against actual*: the planned waves and critical path against the order units merged,
     and each unit's planned Steps against its head rounds.
  6. *Lessons*: one row per signal, each with its source record: every respec (§7.6) with the spec
     section, the unit and the deviation's quoted evidence; every `fix-design` finding; every
     `interface` amendment; a reviewer with no finding kept across the handoff or with the most
     overruled; a unit escalated (§6.4) or stopped at the cap; a decision answered by default. The
     rows are facts from the store; the report proposes nothing.
- **Where it runs.** The integrate skill, after it closes the handoff, runs `postmortem` and tells
  the owner the path and three lines: total wall time, the unit with the most rounds, the reviewer
  with the most overruled findings, then offers the retrospective (§7.7).

### 7.6 Correcting the spec during the build

In 1.2 a spec shown wrong mid-build has one path: a `fix-design` finding voids the approval
(`approve-spec --void`), and the whole spec goes back through `review-spec`, `plan`, `review-plan`
and `file`; the void's `after-void` approval keeps the handoff id (`approve_spec.py:62-68`), but
every unit is re-planned and nothing says which units the change touches. The §6.5 `amend` ruling
restates an Interfaces line only. A wrong behaviour, acceptance value or rule in the spec costs a
full re-run. 1.2.1 adds a respec: a correction of the sections at fault, reviewed and rebuilt in
proportion to its size.

- **The deviation.** §6.5's kinds gain `spec`: the spec states a behaviour, value or rule that the
  code, a predecessor's merged head or a test shows wrong or impossible. Its comment quotes the
  spec line (`path:line` in the frozen spec) and the evidence (a command and its output, or a
  `path:line` in the base). It blocks. The builder orders and the foreman brief define it; the
  foreman raises it as a finding of kind `spec` with the same two parts. A deviation that only
  restates an Interfaces line stays `interface`.
- **The ruling.** `respec` is an option of every `spec` deviation's decision. It is the owner's
  alone: `decision.check_ruling` refuses it when the author starts `judges:`, as `amend` (§6.5),
  and `judges.decision_question` leaves it out. In leavemealone the question waits for the owner.
  The other options stay: `<fid> finding overruled` (the spec stands and the builder follows it),
  `stop the unit`, one more round. `decision rule` writes `respec/R<n>.open.json`
  (`{id: "R<n>", unit, comment_id, at}`, the store's `file` kind) in the handoff's directory, never
  `state.json` (see *Writing it* below). A respec is open while that file exists, no approval
  entry names it, its unit is not stopped and its handoff is the latest approval's; applied once an approval names it, withdrawn when its unit is stopped or a later approval supersedes its handoff, and `approve-spec`, `status` and `integrate` treat a withdrawn respec as not open. The raising unit waits; the other units of the wave go on.
- **From a review's design finding.** A plan or integrate review's `fix-design` finding (a head
  review refuses that disposition, `review.py:664-665`) may open a respec in place of a void:
  `superegm respec open --review <id> --finding <fid> --runcard <card>` (`respec.py`) writes the same
  `respec/R<n>.open.json` with `unit: null` and `review`, `finding` in place of `comment_id`. It is
  the owner's alone, as the ruling above: it refuses when `SUPEREGM_ROLE` is set or the card's mode is
  leavemealone (`respec open: owner only`), and refuses a finding whose disposition is not `fix-design`
  or a head review's finding. `review-plan`'s Step 4 and `integrate` offer it beside the void when they
  meet a `fix-design` finding; a leavemealone panel never offers it. The rest of this section applies
  unchanged; with no raising unit nothing waits but `file` and `integrate`, which refuse while it is
  open (`file: respec R<n> open`), and `next` prints `waiting respec R<n>` (§7.1). Once an approval
  names R<n>, the source review's cycle closes as an `--amend-units` approval closes one
  (`review.py:375-381`): its `fix-design` finding is resolved by the respec, the cycle's exit 4 ends,
  and the next plan or integrate review opens a new cycle on the corrected plan. `approve-spec --void`
  stays for a change too large to respec.
- **The correction.** The owner types `/superegm:review-spec <spec> --respec R<n>`. The skill drafts
  the smallest diff to the spec that settles the deviation, with the owner in a Q&A (one question
  at a time, as the brainstorm), and commits it on the spec's branch. A merged unit the range
  or Interfaces test touches (below) gets a new follow-up row in the same diff (`Follows: U<n>` in
  its title), because a merged head is never rebuilt. Before the commit the skill runs
  `approve-spec --respec R<n> --dry-run`, which runs `respec.touched` (the function the approval
  runs) on the working-tree draft, writes nothing and prints the touched units and
  `merged_review` (below), so a follow-up row the owner wants goes into the same reviewed diff.
- **The review.** `review open --respec R<n>` opens a spec cycle whose evidence is the diff from the
  latest approval's commit to `HEAD` plus the sections it touches, not the whole spec. Its panel is
  `design`, `codebase-details` and the Astra lane, narrowed further by §4.1 as any spec cycle, and
  its cap is 2 (below `review.rounds`). Findings outside the diff's sections are out of remit.
- **The approval.** `approve-spec --respec R<n>` appends an entry of kind `respec` to the latest
  handoff: it copies every frozen field, as `--amend-units`, and records `respec`, the diff's
  changed line ranges in the previous approval's coordinates and `review`. It refuses when the
  cycle has not converged (`approve-spec: respec R<n>: review <id> not converged`), when the latest
  approval is not of this handoff, when a merged unit the range or Interfaces test touches
  has no follow-up row naming it (`approve-spec: respec R<n>: <U> is merged and overlaps <path:a-b>: add a
  follow-up row`), and when the diff changes a line a frozen field reads (the `Rounds:` line or the
  triage section; `approve-spec: respec R<n>: changes the frozen <field>: void and re-approve`), because a respec carries the frozen fields unchanged. The entry
  records the approval's time as the respec's `at`, and in the same command
  writes the touched units below, so no session end leaves a respec approved and unapplied.
- **The units it touches.** `approve-spec --respec`, through `respec.py`, writes `respec/R<n>.json`
  (`touched`, `untouched`, `follow_ups`, `line_map`) in the handoff's directory. `respec/` and the
  comment files are the store's `file` kind, as `decision_comment_*.md` is, so `__init__.py` gains
  no kind and `approve_spec`'s `WRITES` gains `file`:
  - **Coordinates.** Each unit's Design range is read in the coordinates of the approval its
    section was last rendered under and carried forward through the line map of each consecutive
    pair of approved texts (`difflib` over `review.approved_text` of each entry) to the previous
    approval's coordinates, then intersected with the diff's ranges. The same map carries every
    unfiled unit's range to the new approval, so `graph` renders each one, touched or not, at the
    new coordinates.
  - **Shared text.** A unit is also touched, whatever its Design range, when its stored body
    (`unit_<id>.md`, Global constraints included, which `graph` renders apart from the Design
    range) contains verbatim a line the diff removes or changes; a changed range that no unit's
    Design range covers and no body quotes (the preamble, the goals) touches every unfiled and
    every filed, unmerged unit; a change to an Interfaces line touches its producer and every
    unit whose Consumes names it; a prior applied respec's added lines count as part of the body of
    every unit it touched, so a second correction of a corrected line finds the same units. A
    `## Global constraints` line, or a line a body carries under its own `## Global constraints`, always counts however short; any other shared line counts with at least 20
    non-space characters, or with a digit or a backtick (a value or an assertion, such as
    `assert timeout == 3`), and never a fence, a table separator row or a heading marker alone. A merged unit goes through the range and Interfaces
    tests only, and one either touches is the follow-up refusal's; a merged unit only the shared
    text or the uncovered-range test touches is listed in `respec/R<n>.json` as `merged_review`,
    shown when the diff is drafted (above) and printed again by the approval, for the owner to
    add a follow-up row or not.
  - **Writing it.** The marks below are not written to `state.json`, which has no lock and whose
    writers (`launch`, `status`'s wait loop, `inbox`, `close`) replace the whole file, so a written
    mark could be lost. `respec.py` writes only `respec/R<n>.json` before the approval entry, and
    a mark is derived on every read: a unit carries `replan: R<n>` or `respec: R<n>` while it is in
    an applied respec's `touched` and the clearing event below has not happened after the
    respec's `at`, and the unit is not stopped: `status`, `launch` and `integrate` ignore a stopped
    unit's mark. A crash before the
    entry leaves `respec/R<n>.json` without an entry; running `approve-spec --respec` again
    rewrites it and appends the entry. `respec.marks(store, handoff)` is the one reader every
    caller below uses;
  - an unfiled unit touched: `replan: R<n>`. While it stands the unit's converged plan review is
    stale: `gate` treats its body as unreviewed and refuses it (`gate: <U> needs a re-plan after
    respec R<n>`), which `file` and `apply` relay, and `next` routes the handoff to `plan`, then to
    `review-plan`. The plan skill rewrites each marked section from the corrected spec (the one
    exception to keeping sections as they are) and adds a `Corrected: R<n>` line to it; `graph`
    records each unit's `corrected` list from that line in `handoff_units.json`, and the mark
    clears once that list names R<n>. Other renders (`decide`, a `graph` run for other units)
    record nothing and clear nothing. Once cleared, the gate's ordinary rule holds: the body is
    reviewed when a `review-plan` cycle converged on its current sha;
  - a filed, unmerged unit touched: a comment file `respec_comment_<unit>_R<n>.md` quoting the
    diff hunks in its range, and `respec: R<n>`. While the mark stands,
    `status` reports the unit `held: respec R<n>` and offers no merge for its head, however its
    review ended, and neither does `inbox`'s clean-head list or `launch`'s merge offer, each of
    which reads the mark itself; the wave posts the comment as `decision_comment` is and relaunches the unit with
    reason `respec` (§6.5's reasons gain it). `launch_builder` adds every applied respec touching
    the unit to its prompt under `# Spec corrections`, as it adds amendments, and records their ids
    as `respecs` in the launch record at the moment it prepares the prompt, and the PR head it
    leaves as `head` in its exit record; the mark clears when the PR head that `status` saved is a
    commit made after the `started_at` of a launch whose `respecs` name R<n> (whatever its reason;
    the commit time is read as `own_commit` reads it, U31), or when such a launch exited 0 and the
    saved head equals its recorded `head` (a builder that found the head already correct pushes
    nothing), so a launch that fails before pushing leaves it standing, and every later launch names R<n> too, and `prepare` writes to `r<n>/amendments.md`
    exactly the respecs that launch record names (and `prepare.astra_brief` pastes that file, its text ruling over the original contract where they differ), so builder and reviewer hold the same contract.
    A respec approved after the prompt was prepared is in neither, and its mark stands. After posting
    the wave runs `superegm respec posted --respec R<n> --unit <U> --comment <id>`, which writes
    `respec/R<n>.<unit>.posted`; `status` prints `open: respec R<n>` for each open respec, the line
    integrate refuses on; a tick or session repeats the post
    while that file is absent and the relaunch while the mark stands, and `status.tick_trigger`
    returns `respec R<n> <unit>` for a standing mark or an absent `.posted` file, so a driven tick
    starts the `wave` that does it. The filed body
    never changes; the correction rides beside it;
  - a follow-up row: planned and filed as any new row, `Blocked by` the merged unit;
  - an untouched unit: nothing; it keeps building.
- **What it never does.** A respec never changes a filed unit's Scope, Blocked by or DoD command;
  a correction that needs one is a `stop the unit` and a follow-up row. It never reopens a merged
  head. A respec touching the `## Units` table beyond follow-up rows goes through `--amend-units`.
- **Counting it.** The post-mortem's Lessons (§7.5) list every respec; integrate refuses while a
  respec is `open` (`integrate: respec R<n> is open`) or a unit still holds a `respec:` mark
  (`integrate: <U> holds respec R<n>`).

### 7.7 The retrospective

The post-mortem says what happened; nothing in 1.2 changes because of it. 1.2.1 closes the loop
with a retrospective the owner runs after integrate.

- **When.** Integrate, after the post-mortem, offers it once. The owner may also type
  `/superegm:retro [--handoff <id>]` later; by default the latest closed handoff.
- **The Q&A.** The skill reads `postmortem.md` and works through its Lessons with the owner, one
  question at a time and in the brainstorm's manner (§3.7): for each lesson, what caused it and
  what would have prevented it. It asks only where the cause is not plain from the records; a row
  whose fix is obvious gets a proposed edit and no question. The owner may stop at any point.
- **The edits.** Each agreed cause becomes one proposed edit to one of:
  - a reference document (§3.5) under the configured `dir`: an invariant, an architecture fact, a
    rule specs must state (for example a `lessons.md` of spec patterns that needed a respec);
  - a reviewer file (§3.6): a new custom reviewer, or a reworded prompt for one that missed or
    over-raised;
  - `.superegm/config.json`: `review.rounds`, `units.*`, `reviewers.<kind>`, `mode`, `driver.chain`;
  - `.superegm/brainstorm.md`, committed: questions to ask in every brainstorm, which the
    brainstorm reads at its start (U83); not the per-decision brief the brief lookup reads.
  The skill shows each edit as a diff; the owner accepts, changes or drops it. The accepted edits
  land in one commit `retro: <handoff>` on the current branch, and the skill writes
  `<handoff dir>/retro.md` listing each lesson, its cause and what was done.
- **Where it takes effect.** Reference documents, reviewer files and `.superegm/brainstorm.md` are
  frozen by the next spec's approval (§3.1, §3.5, §3.6) and read by the next brainstorm. A config
  key is either live or frozen. Before the owner's yes on a live key (`driver.chain`, `units.*`
  read by `graph`, `reviewers.<kind>` for a review not yet opened) the skill names every open
  handoff and says the edit applies to it at once; on a frozen key (`mode`, `review.rounds`,
  which an approval freezes, §4, §7.1) it reads each open handoff's approval: one whose approval
  froze the key keeps it, and one whose approval has no frozen value (an approval before 1.2.1,
  which falls back to the live `config.mode`) is named as changed at once. A respec's root cause in the spec text
  itself is for the next spec; the finished spec is not edited by the retrospective.
- **What it never does.** It applies nothing without the owner's yes, writes nothing to the
  store but `retro.md`, and never edits the approved spec of any handoff. The skill writes
  `retro.md` through `superegm postmortem --handoff <id> --retro <file>`, which copies the file
  in and needs no run card (`WRITES = frozenset(['file'])`).

### 7.8 The judges rule a builder's contest first

In 1.2 a builder's contest of a head finding (decision point 1 on a head review) goes to the owner in
normal mode and to the judges only in leavemealone. The owner, mid-build, has neither the diff nor
the round's comments in front of them, and in practice rules from the foreman's and builder's
sentences alone. In 1.2.1 the judges rule it first in both modes.

- **What goes to the panel.** On a head review, a high or medium finding the builder contested or
  declined. The question is `superegm judges question --review <id> --round <n>`, its options the
  routing table's (`finding upheld`, `finding overruled`, `redesign — <why>`), its evidence the
  round's `comments.md`, the diff, the unit's body, `r<n>/spec_range.md` and `r<n>/amendments.md`
  (`judges.render_brief` embeds each evidence file's contents, from the panel's snapshots, in the Astra member's request, since that lane reads no paths). The panel sequence,
  the cap (`judges.py` `CAP = 5`) and the routing of a converged option are leavemealone's
  (`skills/judges/references/leavemealone.md`), unchanged, except that `review settle --judges
  <panel>` is accepted on a normal-mode card for a head review, where `check_settle` today
  requires `--owner` (`review.py:679-680`); the ruling journals
  `Ruling (judges <panel>, contest): …` in normal mode.
- **What stays the owner's.** A panel that does not converge: the owner gets point 1's question as
  today, with one added line naming `judges/<panel>/verdict.json` and each judge's last choice. The
  owner-only rulings stay the owner's in both modes: `<fid> allowed`, `amend` (§6.5), `respec`
  (§7.6) and the CI finding the helper never lets the judges settle. A spec, plan or integrate
  review's contest is unchanged: normal mode asks the owner, since the owner is at the review.
- **Under the chain.** The panel runs in the phase that hit the contest; a converged ruling lets the
  chain go on. An unconverged one stays that unit's open point 1 decision with the verdict line,
  listed by `inbox`, as §7.2 keeps a unit's decision; the wave goes on with its other units, and
  only that unit waits. `next wait` stays for the phases before filing.
- **No new key, nothing hidden.** A converged ruling stands: `decision rule` refuses a second ruling
  on the same decision (`<id> already ruled`, `decision.py:482`), and this section does not change
  that. The wave's report names every judges' contest ruling with its panel, and an owner who
  disagrees acts on the unit as on any other (`stop the unit`, or a ruling on the next round).

## 9. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Chained phases run as unattended `bypassPermissions` sessions. | Only in a store the owner said yes for in the SDLC step, which sets `driver.chain` to `true` and lets review-spec's approval step write the env file, register the store and install the driver; a store registered under 1.2 keeps 1.2's driver. `doctor` prints `chain: on` or `chain: off` for this repository's store; another registered store shows it in its own repository. |
| The driver's env file holds a credential, and the setup runs from a skill. | The brainstorm writes nothing outside the repository; review-spec, typed and external, writes it through `superegm driver env` from the helper's environment, never the transcript, mode `0600`, keeping an existing file unless the owner agreed to replace it, refusing a profile with `SubagentStart` hooks; `register` is skipped for a registered store, so its `root` never moves to a spec worktree. |
| A chained phase fails, or exits without moving on, every tick. | A launch that leaves `next` unchanged is a failed launch; two consecutive failures stop the handoff (§7.1), as 1.2 does for `wave`; a typed run or `driver reset` clears it. |
| A stopped or stalled plan review restarts with a fresh cap. | `next` prints `waiting review-plan: … stopped`; a driven session that meets a stall writes a wait record; only the owner's typed run resumes either. |
| Idle sessions while heads wait for a merge. | The `wave` phase keeps `tick_trigger`; nothing to do starts nothing. |
| A builder writes the instructions its judges or reviewers read. | Every role of a handoff reads instruction files by their frozen blob shas (§3.1); `approve-spec` prints each file that changed since the previous approval. |
| An instruction file tells a builder to open a channel (Relay) that then carries text into the session. | SuperEGM reads nothing from it (anti-goal 3); the owner writes the file and grants the tool, and owns what the channel delivers. |
| A medium is deferred wrongly. | A zero-context triage goldfish answers the fields without being given the patterns; `confirmed` needs a `file:line` at the head; regressions, test-delta, owner-only and external findings are ineligible; every deferral is listed in the review comment and undone by the owner's `finding upheld` ruling, which also turns triage off for the unit. |
| The triage goldfish reads the spec's patterns in the review worktree and shapes its answers to them. | Accepted: its adapter forbids reading the spec and `docs/specs/`, but nothing enforces it; every deferral is in the review comment for the owner to undo. |
| The foreman transcribes the triage reply, or dispatches again until one matches. | Accepted, as for reviewer findings files today; the recorder refuses a second triage of a round, and every deferral is in the review comment for the owner to undo. |
| A config change breaks an in-flight handoff. | `rounds`, `triage`, `mode` and `instructions` are frozen per approval and carried by `--amend-units`; a cycle keeps the cap it opened with. The size limits are read live: a lowered limit can refuse a reviewed plan's unfiled unit, and the owner re-plans; accepted, since filed units are never checked again. |
| A driven review-plan running rounds for hours holds the store's tick slot: `tick_live` refuses a typed `wave` on any handoff of the store (`TICK_LIVE`), and other handoffs' driven waves wait. | Accepted: 1.2's one-`.git` rule stays; the owner can `driver reset` or wait for the session to end. |
| The registered root is behind `<base>`, diverged, dirty or on another branch, so a driven plan reads the wrong spec or code. | Before filing `next` prints `waiting checkout: <root>: <reason>` unless the root is on `<base>` at the fetched commit with clean tracked files; the owner updates the checkout. |
| The fetch fails (network, credentials). The tick's fetch races a session starting. | `waiting fetch` for every handoff of the store; nothing launches there until a fetch succeeds; one journal line per distinct error. The tick fetches into its own `refs/superegm/tick-base`, never the `refs/superegm/base` a session reads. |
| An owner narrows a spec panel mid-cycle to drop a reviewer whose findings are unwelcome. | The narrowing comes from the owner's own invocation or config, is journaled and printed, applies from the next unrecorded round only, never widens, and keeps `design`. |
| A cap of 3 moves work into cap rulings. | `review.rounds` and a spec's `Rounds:` raise it per project. |

## 12. Alternatives Considered

- The precheck as a full head panel run locally before the push: rejected. It doubles the head
  review's cost on every push; one self-review catches much of it, and the head panel still runs.
- The precheck enforced by `record-launch` (a push refused until `precheck` passes): rejected. A
  check the builder cannot satisfy would block the unit; listing open findings in the PR body
  keeps the head review the judge.
- A model switch on every retry, as single-task agents do: rejected. A fix round resumes the same
  session (§6.2), and a model change forfeits that; escalation waits for a second failing round.
- Count a round where a reviewer did not run toward the cap, so no cycle stalls (plan, head and
  integrate): rejected. `proceed` (§4.2) ends a stall at the owner's word without changing what a
  completed round means.

- **One key per audience** (`builder.orders_file`, `context_file`, …) for instructions. Rejected:
  every new audience would add a key and a code path; one list with `to` covers them all.
- **Instruction files read at the unit's base or the pull-request head.** Rejected: the head is the
  builder's to write, and the base moves as units land, so a builder's merged edit would reach later
  units' reviewers; the frozen shas are neither.
- **Triage rules in `config.json`.** Rejected by the owner: the rules decide what may merge with a
  known defect, so they belong in the reviewed, approved spec, frozen at approval.
- **Declarable triage fields and per-severity required lists.** Rejected: one known rule set needs
  four fixed fields; a general grammar costs a parser and a review lane for no user.
- **Triage that may unblock a high.** Rejected by the owner (anti-goal 1).
- **The foreman as triager.** Rejected: it drives its unit to land, so its answers would decide its
  own merge. The owner or the panel per finding was rejected too: that is the per-finding argument
  triage exists to end.
- **Head reviewers answer the fields on their own rows.** Rejected: the reviewer who found a medium
  would grade its own finding's reach, and `verified` is worth something only from a second reader.
- **A command that re-fixes a cycle's panel** (`review panel --drop`). Rejected: the config list is
  already the owner's panel choice; a second place to set it would need its own ruling grammar.
- **Reviewers recorded `--not-run` with an owner reason count as run.** Rejected: an owner reason on
  each round per dropped reviewer is busywork, and `not run` would stop meaning a reviewer failed.
- **Keep the cap fixed at 5.** Rejected by the owner: the right number is per project.
- **Chain only in leavemealone.** Rejected by the owner: the phases after approval are mechanical in
  either mode; normal mode differs only in who answers a decision, and stops once at file's Create.
- **A normal-mode chain that files without asking.** Rejected: Create is the owner's decision in
  normal mode (point 4); skipping it would make the chain change the mode.
- **A SessionStart hook in the builder profile for start-of-session commands.** Rejected: the
  profile holds no hooks (1.2 §4, `launch_builder.py:347-362`), and an instruction file records what
  the builder was told.
- **A setup that knows Relay.** Rejected: Relay is not part of SuperEGM. `--mcp` and `--allow`
  cover any server and any command.
- **Probing on every `doctor` run.** Rejected: the probe is a model call, and it may register
  sessions on a channel.
- **The chain on without the owner's yes.** Rejected by the owner: a store registered under 1.2 was
  registered for driven waves only, so chaining must be the owner's choice per store; 1.2.1 asks
  once, in the SDLC workflow step (§3.4).
- **`approve-spec` registers the store.** Rejected: registration is the owner's opt-in to unattended
  sessions in that repository, given once in the SDLC step's area 7 and carried out by review-spec's
  approval step (§3.4), never by the helper on its own.
- **A changed-lines limit that refuses.** Rejected: the line count is known only after the build;
  it stays planning guidance.
- **A `max_unit_deps` limit.** Rejected: no evidence ties the dependency count to relaunches, and a
  unit consuming three interfaces has no split that meets it.
- **The helper merges shared-file units in order (a merge queue).** Rejected: merging is the
  owner's (non-goal), and an App merge would need a bypass the builder must not have.
- **The helper rebases a conflicting branch itself.** Rejected: only a unit's builder pushes to
  its branch (1.2), and a conflict needs judgement about both units' intent.
- **Relaunch a later unit whenever a shared-file predecessor lands**, to keep every head current.
  Rejected: a launch and a review per landing even with no conflict; GitHub's `mergeable` already
  says which heads need one.
- **One worktree per unit kept across rounds**, so a resumed builder's paths stay valid. Rejected:
  the per-round worktrees are the audit trail and what `clean` removes (1.2); one line in the
  resumed prompt marks the old paths stale.
- **A key to turn resuming off.** Not added: the fresh fallback covers every failure the design
  knows of; a key waits for evidence that a resume does worse.
- **A chain driven by every event (a pull request's change, a CI result) through a GitHub webhook.**
  Rejected for 1.2.1: it needs a listener reachable from GitHub. The kick covers the events
  SuperEGM itself causes, and the 5-minute interval bounds the rest (§7.4).
- **The chain on by default for every store, a 1.2 store included.** Rejected: a store registered
  under 1.2 would start unattended `bypassPermissions` phases on a plugin update with no word from
  the owner.
- **A Relay or chat answer instead of the inbox.** Rejected by anti-goal 3: a ruling read from chat
  has no owner login to check.
- **Trim the current 38-unit plan and file it.** Rejected by the owner. That plan was written under
  the rules §6.5 replaces, and planning 1.2.1 again from this spec is the first test of the new rules.
- **Downgrade a readiness finding that mentions a fixture, conftest or a line cite.** Rejected.
  Real defects cite lines: in round 3 of the 1.2.1 plan review, U45's mode order and U43's missing
  repo root. A finding's severity comes from its requirement and failure scenario, not its words.
- **Refuse every code cite and lower the fenced-share warning.** Rejected. A cite is useful
  evidence. Only a cite on an Interfaces or Produces line binds a consumer to a line that moves.
- **Drop a finding mechanically when it falls outside Scope or the Design range.** Rejected. In
  1.2, round 3 of head-U22 found a second ruling writer outside Scope, and it was a real defect.
  Severity follows the consequence, and widening Scope is one ruling.
- **Let the foreman defer a defect already on the base.** Rejected. It would decide its own
  merge, the triager §12 rejects. Such a finding is ruled like any other.
- **A versioned plan format.** Rejected. The Step grammar that `body.py`, `launch_builder.py`,
  `tdd_order.py` and `collect.py` parse stays, so old and new plans read the same.

- **The older superpowers checklist model or a team brainstorm on by default.** Not taken by the
  owner. The 7.0.0 conversation already asks one question at a time and plays the design back;
  what it lost in SuperEGM was the builder's read and the pictures, which §3.7 restores.
- **A lighter respec: record the correction and add a follow-up row.** Rejected by the owner. The
  wrong behaviour would ship first and be fixed after, and the running units would keep building
  against it.
- **An automatic retrospective.** Rejected by the owner. Reference documents and reviewers bind
  every later agent; they change with the owner's yes, after a Q&A where the cause is not plain.
- **Keep a contest the owner's in normal mode, judges only in leavemealone.** Rejected by the owner:
  a contest mid-build needs the diff and the round's comments, which the panel reads and the owner
  does not have in hand.
- **A config key to choose who rules a contest.** Not taken: the owner sees every judges' ruling in
  the wave's report and can stop the unit, and a key would be one more setting to explain.

team: off (the brainstorm's cartographer and team subagents were not run; the owner brainstormed in
conversation)
